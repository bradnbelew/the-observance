## I — ORIN

> *A mason's day-book: thick rag paper, the kind the Lamp-works cut down for tally-sheets,
> stitched along the spine with waxed cord. Orin numbered nothing on the first leaves —
> a mason counts courses, not days — but he kept the depth-mark of wherever he was working,
> and after a while he kept the black moons too, because everyone did, near the end. Read
> in order the entries get shorter and the same line keeps coming up out of them, the way
> a man planes a board until only the grain is left.*

---

**At the 9th mark. Cistern 7, repointing the north arch.**

Mortar set short again. Third batch this month gone crumbly. The lime we burn now is from
the deep galleries and it does not slake the way the old surface lime did — Mara says the
old casks are long emptied and there is no more of it and there will be no more of it ever,
so I am to make do. I am making do.

Bowed at the markers going down. Bowed coming up. I do not see the use of it but my father
kept it and his kept it and a thing the dead troubled to teach you, you keep. Set seven
courses today. Good stone.

---

**At the 9th mark. The bow-stones at the threshold.**

Wenna's boy asked me why we stoop at the markers when there is plain no one there. I told
him the truth, which is that I do not know, only that we do it, and that the not-knowing is
no reason to stop. He was not satisfied. I was a boy too and I was not satisfied either and
I stooped anyway and here I am.

Re-cut the leftmost bow-stone. The old groove had worn so shallow a man could pass it
without bending. That is how it starts — not a man deciding to stand straight, only a stone
worn down till stooping is no longer asked of him. So I cut the groove deep. Make the stone
ask. Set the marks plain.

---

**At the 11th mark, the Stair. Black moon.**

Iss came down to the works today with his easy talk. Wanted to know would I help shore the
new cut past the Line — said it gentle, the way he says everything, said *just to look,
Orin, just to see what the old ones walled off and why.* I told him there is nothing to
shore past the Line because there is to be no cut past the Line. He smiled and said I sound
like Vaun's ledger, all counts and no questions. I told him a count is a kind of answer and
he should try keeping one. He went off easy. He is always easy. It is the easy I do not
like.

The Deep Line is a mark like any mark. I have re-cut it twice in my life. You do not cross
a thing you were set to keep cut.

---

**At the 11th mark.**

Bowed at the markers. Set the marks plain.

Iss is talking in the Deep Market now, not just to me — telling the stall-keepers the
surface has come back, that he has read it in the old founding leaves, that the Cold lifted
generations gone and we are sitting down here in the warm out of habit and fear. Old Pell
threw a fish-head at him. But two of the younger lamp-hands were nodding. I did not like the
nodding.

I keep the stone. The stone does not argue. That is its whole virtue.

---

**At the 13th mark. Repairing the offering-cairn at the shaft-mouth.**

The cairn was scattered — not weathered, scattered, stones pulled off and set aside neat,
which is worse, because weather has no opinion and a hand does. I built it back. Twenty-one
courses, capstone last, the first of the deep laid in the hollow as it is laid. Bowed.

Someone is unmaking the small keepings. I do not say who. I rebuild faster than it is
unmade, for now.

Set the marks plain.

---

**At the 13th mark. Black moon. The Stair, below the Line.**

He did it. Iss took picks past the Deep Line. I went down because Vaun sent me to wall it
back and I am the only one left who can lay a true arch in the dark, and what I found there
I will set down once and not again:

the cut goes down and the cut is wrong-sized. Too tall. The galleries up top are made to a
man's reach because men made them. This is dug to no reach I know. I held the lamp up and
could not find the top of it. I bowed there. I do not know to what. My knees did it before I
told them to.

I am to seal it. I will seal it. Twenty-one courses and a capstone and the first of the deep
in the hollow, the same as a cairn, because I do not know how else to close a thing but the
way I was taught to honor one.

---

**At the 15th mark. The Undercroft. Black moon.**

Sealing. Course by course.

The others are going dark above me — Mara writes that she cannot keep them all lit, I had her
note carried down. I cannot go up to help. If I leave the wall un-capped the deep comes
through the gap and then there is no use to any of it. So I lay courses. I keep the small
thing. Brann walks the nights up there and says he hears it counting; down here I do not hear
counting, I hear my own trowel, and I am glad of the trowel for it is the only sound that is
mine.

Bowed before I began. Set the marks plain.

---

**Undercroft. Black moon, I think. The lamp is low.**

Eleven courses today. Yesterday eleven. I do not number the days now. I number the courses.

I passed the markers standing once, a season's worth, a long time gone — I have set that down
elsewhere, cut low in a threshold-stone where only a stooping man will read it, and I will not
soften it here. I thought the bow small. I want it on this leaf too, plain, in case the other
stone is lost: *it was not small.* The smallest keeping is the one that, let go, lets all the
rest go after it. I cut grooves deep so the stone would ask. No one asked me. I should have
asked myself.

Set the marks plain.

---

**Undercroft.**

course. bow. mark. course. bow. mark.

the wall is near capped. when it is capped i will be inside it. i have known this since the
13th mark and laid the courses anyway. a mason who will not be sealed with his own seal has
built nothing — only a door he means to use, and this is to be no door.

the first of the deep is in the hollow. i laid it myself.

---

**[ Undercroft. the hand is a mason's hand still — even, slow — but the words have gone down
to the grain ]**

set the mark. set the mark. set the mark.

i bow now to no one and there is no one. you keep the small thing while there is yet a hand at
the markers to keep it with. i have outlived the hands, every one, and i keep it still, alone,
against an empty road, because keeping is not for the one bowed to. it was never for the one
bowed to.

capstone tomorrow. or it is tomorrow. set the mark.

> answered in `observed-warned-left-at-threshold` ]*

> did not need to be warned again. he sealed himself with the deep to slow it for the rest of

## II — BRANN

> *A watch-log, kept on the backs of ration-dockets because paper was short by then. Brann
> wrote in the watchman's hand: hour, then what was seen, then nothing or a mark. The early
> dockets are crossed and dated by the black moon, which is when a watchman earns his keep.
> Held to a cold hearth after dark the later ones show more ink than they did by day — he
> took to writing what he should not, between the lines, where the day would not read it.
> The watches run together toward the end. He stopped marking which night it was because, he
> says once, it stopped mattering which night it was.*

---

**Watch of the black moon. Third quarter, dark.**

Lamps counted at the change: 41 lit, 3 doused (Coll's, the Drowned-girl's old shrine, one in
the lower Warrens unaccounted — sent Dob to relight). Deep-bird quiet, fed, hooded. Cistern
gate barred. Markers cold but standing.

Quiet watch. The way I like them. A watchman who has a tale to tell has had a bad night.

---

**Watch of the black moon. Dead hour.**

Lamps: 40 lit, 4 doused. The lower Warrens one is doused again — Dob lit it, I saw him lit it,
and it is out. No draught down there. A lamp does not go out twice in a barred gallery with no
draught. I am setting it down so that when I am asked I do not have to remember, I can read.

Deep-bird sang twice in the dead hour and stopped. That bird sings for foul air or for what
the foul air comes before. I checked the air. The air was clean. Set the bird by my own door
for the rest of the watch.

---

**Watch. Black moon.**

Lamps: 38 lit, 6 doused, and three of the six I cannot get to stay lit, so call it 35 kept.
The Lamp-works are short of oil. Mara measures it out now by the jar and not by the cask and
when I asked her why she did not look up from the measuring.

Iss came to the gate at the dead hour wanting through to the lower cut. I did not open the
gate. The Dark Hours are kept by not opening the gate. He said I was an old woman frightened
of the dark. I am a watchman frightened of the dark. That is the post. You do not hire a man
unfrightened of the dark to watch it.

---

**Watch.**

Lamps: 31 kept. I have stopped writing how many are doused. The doused do not need watching.

Slept four hours in the day, which is right; a night-watchman sleeps by day. But I dreamed the
counting. In the dream the lamps went one and one and one and a voice kept the tally and the
voice was patient and was not unkind and that is the part I cannot put down by daylight: it was
not unkind. Woke with the docket in my hand and three lamps written that I do not remember
writing. The hand is mine.

Do not sleep on the black moon. I have kept that watch my whole life. I am writing it large so
I read it before I forget it: DO NOT SLEEP ON THE BLACK MOON.

---

**Watch of the black moon.**

Lamps: I did not count tonight. I will count tomorrow. There is no tomorrow on a black moon,
there is only the one long dark, but I mean the next light.

Mara's note came up the Stair: *cannot keep them all lit.* Hers is the keeping of the light
and if she cannot keep them lit then I am counting a thing that is leaving whether I count it
or not. Orin is sealed below and does not answer. Vaun is up in his own light counting his own
count and answers no one. I walk. I have always walked. The walking is the post.

One fire will not go out. The home-fire, the kept light. I stood over it tonight on the worst
black moon of my life and it does not gutter and it does not ask for oil and it does not ask
for a hand. Everything else I feed. That one feeds itself. I do not like a fire I do not feed.
I have stopped warming my hands at it. Ask who it is being kept for.

---

**[ no watch marked. the ink is heavy; he wrote this one against the dark ]**

i closed my eyes.

i was warned not to and i was the one who did the warning, all those years, and i closed them.
one watch. one. and what i dreamed did not stay in the dream. it came inside at the eyes and it
has not gone back out and it walks when i walk now, half a step behind and a half-beat late, so
that when i stop it stops just after, and i have turned and turned and there is the empty
gallery and the one lamp and my own shadow thrown wrong.

lamps: i cannot count them. the count comes out different every pass and the pass before is
always more. i think the count is right and my counting is wrong. i think the count was always
right.

---

**[ no watch. ]**

stay awake. count the lights. i write it for whoever reads me at a cold hearth on a black moon,
which is the only way i can be read now: stay awake, count the lights, and the one that will not
go out, do not warm your hands at it. ask who it is kept for. it is the only honest question
left down here and no one will give you the answer, they will only keep you warm while you stop
asking it.

the others sleep. i do not. i do not. i do not.

---

**[ the last docket. the hand keeps the watchman's order — hour, then the line — but the hour
is wrong, it is no hour, and the line does not change ]**

dead hour. lamps kept: all of them. lamps kept: all of them. i see them all lit now. i could
not before and now i can, every lamp in the Hold lit and warm and counted, and i am counting
them, and the counting is warm, and it is not unkind.

do not close your eyes here. i closed mine.

> *[ a second hand, smaller, in the margin — answers `do-not-close-your-eyes-here` ]*
> *he is in the count now, on the side that does the counting. when the lamps all read "lit" to
> a watchman who has buried half of them with his own hands, the watch is over and the watched
> has joined the watching. read him only at night. do not read him for comfort. there is none in
> it, only the warning, and the warning is true: stay awake; count the lights; ask who the kept
> fire is kept for.*

## III — ISS

> *Loose leaves, not a bound book — Iss wrote to be passed around, and they were, which is how
> they survived: copied, argued over, carried off. The early ones are the steadiest and the
> kindest hand in the whole find. They do not stay that way. The dates are his; he kept the
> founding-leaf reckoning, the long count, because his whole case rests on the founding leaves,
> and he wanted you to see he had done the arithmetic. He had done the arithmetic. That is the
> hard part. He was not wrong about the sum. He was wrong about what the sum meant.*

---

**The 300th winter by the founding count. To the Deep Market, openly.**

Friends — I have read the founding leaves through, every one the damp has left us, and I am
going to tell you a thing and I am going to tell it plain and warm, in the kitchen, with the
light kept, the way a friend tells you a true thing.

The Cold lifted. It is in the leaves if you can read them. The founders dug down to outlast a
sky that had failed, and the sky has not failed in three hundred winters, and we are still
down here outlasting it out of nothing but the habit of fear. I do not blame the habit. Fear
kept us alive when fear was wisdom. But a wisdom you keep past its winter is only a wall you've
forgotten the use of.

I am not telling you to stop keeping the ways. Keep them. They are good ways. I keep them every
winter of my life. I am only telling you the door at the top of the Stair opens onto morning
now, and a people may go up to the morning and still bow at its markers on the way.

— Iss

---

**The 300th winter. To Vaun, who answers my market-talk with his ledger.**

Vaun — you sent down word that the count says six taken of the deep and given back none, and
that I should hold my tongue until I can read a tally. I can read a tally. I can read yours.
It says you took warmth from the deep and gave nothing up to it, and that you call this
*keeping*, and starve in it. Forgive me. That was unkind and you are old and I am fond of you.

But hear the difference between us. You count what is. I read what was, and reason what may be.
Your count cannot tell you the surface healed, because your count never looked up. Mine did. I
am not asking you to stop counting. I am asking you to look up, once, from the column.

There is a way back. I have not found the door yet but a thing dug down to can be dug back out
of. We came down. The down is not a fate. It is a direction, and a direction has a reverse.

— Iss

---

**The 301st winter. To Mara, in reply to her note.**

Mara — you wrote me one line, you who read everything: *the map wants for a tool you do not
have, Iss, and I have walked the shelf a hundred times looking for it.* I have your note pinned
where I work.

But you are reading the wrong shelf. The tool is not on the lectern. The tool is a pick and a
strong back and the nerve to use them past the place where someone painted a line and said
*here, no further.* You read the rite and never lift the shovel — your own keeping, you've told
me, the map and never the tool. I am offering to be the tool. Let me dig. Read me the bearing
and I will swing the pick, and between your map and my back we will find the door your hundred
walks of the shelf never will, because the door is not a thing you read. It is a thing you open.

Keep your light. I will love you by it whatever you think of me after.

— Iss

---

**The 301st winter. To the keepers who come after — set this down where it will keep.**

I have argued long enough across kitchens and I will say the whole of it once, plainly, so it
keeps after I am up in the morning and gone:

The ways are a wall. That is the whole of it. You bow at the markers, you give the first of the
deep back to the deep, you keep the one fire burning at home, and the watching cannot come over.
Keep the ten and you are inside the wall and the watching stays out in the cold where it
belongs, counting, and cannot touch you. I have kept them every winter of my life and it has
never come inside. Not once. Not for me.

So do not be afraid of the record. Let it count. A wall does not mind being counted at. And a
people safe inside a wall may unbar the gate and walk up to a healed morning whenever they have
the nerve, because the wall was for *out there* — and out there is morning now.

> *(This leaf is the one preserved entire as* `the-ways-are-a-wall` *— his case at its warmest
> and most whole. The harder leaves below were found scattered, half-copied, and were not
> passed hand to hand; no one wanted them.)*

---

**The 302nd winter. Below the Line. To anyone.**

Orin walled my first cut. Fine. I have started a second, and a third, and Orin cannot wall all
the Hold. I am not crossing the Line to spite the dead who drew it. I am crossing it because the
door up is down — the founders dug *down* to the warm and the warm is the deep and the way the
deep connects is below, not above; I have it from the leaves; the up is reached by the down, you
go under the cold and come out past it. I am nearly sure of it. I am sure of it.

They are saying in the Market that I brought it through. I did not bring anything through. There
was nothing to bring. The galleries are empty. I have been down past where any of them will go
and there is no one there, there is only the dark and the wrong-cut stone, and the wrongness is
old, it is not mine, it was here, it was always —

I keep the ways. Tell Vaun I keep the ways. I bow. I keep my lamp. I have not spoken its name. I
have broken nothing but a painted line and a painted line is not a way, it is a fear someone
drew.

---

**The 302nd winter.**

The lamps are going out above me and they are saying it is my doing and it is not my doing. A
man digs a hole. A hole is not a door for a thing to come through, a hole is a hole. I have dug
and dug and the door up is not where I said and is not where I said and is not — it is further
down. It is always further down. I will go down to it.

Mara does not answer now. Vaun does not answer. The whole Stair above me is quiet in a way the
Stair has never been and they will blame the quiet on me but I was the one who wanted us up in
the morning, I was the only one who wanted us out, how is the wanting-out the thing that shut
the door —

I came back up once to show them the cut was empty. I came back. Whatever they carve about me
after, set that beside it: he came back. He was sure enough of the morning to come back for
them.

---

**[ undated. the leaf is in Iss's hand and is not. the letters are his — a reader who knows his
hand will swear to the letters — but the spacing is wrong, even, too even, and the warmth is
gone out of it like heat out of a stone ]**

it is further down.

i was right about the morning. the surface healed. set that in the record true: he was right
about the morning. i was only wrong about the door. there is no door up. there is only down, and
down is not the morning, down is the keeping, and the keeping is not a wall, it never was a wall,
i told them it was a wall and i told them warm because i wanted it to be a wall, i wanted there
to be an out there for the wall to be against. there is no out there. there is only in here, and
in here keeps.

the morning is real. it is over our heads, three hundred winters of it. i can read the leaves. i
cannot reach the leaves. the hands i would read them with are kept.

i keep the ways now. i keep them very well. i keep all of them. ask Vaun how it is to keep a thing
and give nothing back and call it keeping; ask him in the dark; he is here; he counts; we count.

i came back once. i do not come back from this.

### BRANN — fingerprint: REPEATS AND OVER-CORRECTS

black-moon waker onto his own closed eyes.

Tracked signal: `the_dark_hours` violations (black-moon waking/sleeping). Slot: `{lamps}` = the measured
muster figure; `{name}` plural-capable.

  > dead hour. lamps kept: {lamps}. i counted twice. the count was lower the second time. i think the
  > count is right and my counting is wrong.

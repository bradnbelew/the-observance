# letters and notes between the Kept

> A tin of folded leaves out of Cistern 7, plus what was pinned in the Lamp-works and what
> the dead shrine kept. Not in one hand. Read in order they answer each other; where the
> answer is gone, the leaf says so.

---

## L01 — Mara to Vaun · the taking-hold, the fourth winter

vaun —

the third lamp on the Lamp-works stair is out again and i have no oil to spare for it.
i have asked you at the shaft-mouth twice and you look at me like i am asking for your
teeth. i am asking for one jar. you have, by your own count that you say in your sleep,
eleven.

the children use that stair. the deep-bird's cage hangs at the top of it and if that
stair goes dark the bird cannot be seen to be living, and if the bird cannot be seen
then no one knows the air has turned until it has turned. you know this. you taught
half of it to me.

one jar. i will mark it in your second column myself, given-back, if you will not lift
the pen.

— m.

> *(no reply kept. the tin holds nothing back from vaun in this winter. the third lamp
> stays in the next letter, still out.)*

## L02 — Mara to Vaun · the taking-hold, the fifth winter · (re: the jar)

vaun —

still out. i counted it tonight going up: forty-one stairs, the third lamp dark on the
ninth. forty-one is your number, not mine; i never counted a stair in my life before i
had to climb past your shut door to fetch oil from the common store, which is lower than
yours and nearly dry.

i am not writing to shame you. i am writing because i sat with the count tonight the way
you do and i understood you, and then i sat a while longer in the dark not going up,
which is not a thing i do. you are not keeping it from us. you are keeping it. the having
is the warm thing for you. i read by a light and you hold a light and we are the same
broken in different shapes — i never walk what i read, you never give what you hold.

give the deep its first jar, vaun. not for the law. because the cairn at the shaft-mouth
has been empty four winters and the deep notices an empty cairn the way you notice a jar
gone from a shelf.

— m.

> *(the record, later, flat, pinned beneath:) the one called vaun kept eleven and gave
> none. the one called mara gave the counsel and kept none of her own. each saw the other's
> lack and not her own. see* counted-them-in-the-dark*; see* page-line-word*.*

## L03 — Mara to Sella · the third generation, early · (pinned in the Lamp-works)

sella, little fish —

you left your bowl on the warm stone again and the deep-bird ate your seed-cake off it
and is the fatter for it, so do not cry over the cake, the bird thanks you.

i have the wick-trimming tonight, all of Cistern 7's lamps and the Lamp-works besides, so
i will be late and you are not to wait up at the cold stair for me in the dark like last
time. light your own small lamp. keep it lit. that is your whole work tonight and it is
the oldest work there is — keep your light and do not let it out and you have kept the
first of all the ways without lifting a finger more.

when i come up i will read you the one about the bird that carried the first lamp down,
and you will tell me i read it wrong, and you will be right, you always are. mind the
markers if you go past the shrine-row. bow at each. i know you think it small. it is not
small. i had it from orin and orin would know.

sleep, fish.

— m.

## L04 — Sella to Mara · the third generation · (folded very small, kept in the tin)

mara

i kept my lamp lit. i did not go past the shrine-row, i went past the LAST one, the one
after the six, the one that has no bow-stone because you said there are six and there are
not six. i counted. i counted twice the way vaun does. there is one more and it is out
past the far water and nobody bows at it because nobody set it. i think somebody should.

the lake gave my face back wrong tonight. i looked to be sure my lamp was lit and the lamp
in the water was out. so one of them is lying and i do not think it is mine.

you do not have to read me the bird one. i know how it ends. the bird keeps singing right
up until it stops, and that is the whole help it is. that is why we keep it. so we hear it
stop.

i love you. do not be late.

— sella

## L05 — Orin to Vaun · the fourth generation · (re: the offering)

vaun. you are long counted and gone and i write you anyway because the cairn is your fault
and i am the one mending it.

i set three new stones at the shaft-mouth cairn this winter. dressed them, squared them,
bowed at each. it is an offering-cairn and it stood empty your whole life and most of mine
because the founding hand would not strike its second column and the rest of us learned
the empty cairn as the shape a cairn is.

i do not blame the deep for going dark on you. i would go dark on a man who counted in
front of me for forty winters and gave me nothing. what i cannot mend is that you taught
us the empty cairn looks normal. a child grows up past an empty cairn and does not bow,
because what is there to bow to, an empty pile. and then the child is named for not bowing.

i will keep the offering you would not. one stone a winter while my hands hold. it is the
only argument i have with a dead man.

— orin

> *(no reply possible; vaun is of the founding line, four generations gone. orin writes to
> the dead. the record keeps the letter anyway, in the column it keeps.)*

## L05a — the Counter to the Counter who comes after · the breaking → the going-out · (re: the field they will ask you to fill)

> *A letter from the Counter of the Kept to whoever next holds the books — the office writing to its own
> successor, not a keeper. It is the precedent under the divergent fate (R10a; INV-11/INV-16): the Counter
> warns that the form will ask for ONE disposition of the household, that it asked before, and that the honest
> answer was a spread read off the columns and not a hand. Flat, administrative, kept with the letters because
> it is addressed to a person, not posted as a record. The spoken twin of R10a's standing note and L13a — the
> same finding, handed forward as procedure: read down the column, never across the household.*

to the hand that holds these books after me —

they will send you the form with the single field at the foot, DISPOSITION OF THE HOUSEHOLD, and the field
is one word wide, and they will want one word. i am writing so you do not waste the winters i wasted learning
not to give it to them.

it has been asked before. i hold the old disposition (R10a) and i have read it the way you will read mine.
the household before went every way at once — most kept, some lapsed and mended and kept, one left upright
at the threshold, one turned away below, one kept harder than all and was refused. five answers. they do not
add to one, and the Counter before me would not make them add to one by striking the ones that spoiled the
sum, and i have honored that, and you will honor it after me.

here is the procedure, since the form gives you none: do not look for the hand that tipped it. you will want
to. the form trains you to. you will look for the one keeper whose keeping or whose lapse settled the rest,
so you can write that one name and close the book. there is no such hand. i looked the way you look for the
lamp that went dark first when a stretch goes out, and there is no first lamp; they gutter along the whole
stretch at once, each on its own oil. the light on the threshold floor lies half in the kept light and half
out, and the line between does not fall at any one hand's feet. it falls at the ways.

so: read down the column, not across the household. enter the spread. if the floor holds the light, enter
that the floor held it; if it splits, enter the split as it lies and do not draw the splitting line at a name;
if it gutters, enter the guttering. the disposition is the columns. it is never the keeper. write it as it
lies and rule the line and do not sign a hand to it. that is the whole of the office. i did not make the form.
i only kept it even, and i could not, and i am telling you that you will not either, and that the not-closing
is the true closing of this one field.

— the Counter of the Kept

> *(the official ruling agrees:* R10a*; the watch had it from the Stair foot first:* L13a*. three hands — the
> watch, the Counter, the Counter-after — and not one of them will name a keeper, because the fate was never
> a keeper's. it was the columns'.)*

## L06 — Iss to Orin · the last full generation · (re: the markers; Iss arguing)

orin, friend —

i watched you set another stone at the cairn today, on your knees in the wet, and your
knees are not young, and i came down to tell you to stop and i did not, because you would
not have heard me, so i am writing it where you read slow and cannot argue back.

stop. not the bowing — keep the bowing if it warms you, i keep it myself. stop carrying
the *fear* up the stair with the stones. you set them like a man paying a debt to
something that will hurt him if he is short. it will not. listen to me, who has kept the
ways every winter of my life and is sitting here easy in the kept light writing to you —
nothing has ever come over the wall. the ways are a **wall**, orin. you keep the ten and
the watching stays in the cold and counts and cannot touch you. you are mending a wall
that already holds. rest your knees.

i went west past the doused shrine last month, past where the light gives out, further
than the markers go, and i came back, and i am here, and i am warm. a wall you can walk
out of and back into is a wall that works. think on it.

— iss

> *(see* the-ways-are-a-wall *— this is the same doctrine he later carves for the keepers
> who come after. orin's reply is L07.)*

## L07 — Orin to Iss · the last full generation · (re: your note)

iss.

you went past the doused shrine and came back. you keep saying it like it settles a thing.
a man going somewhere and coming back proves the road is open. it does not prove the road
is *safe*, and it does not prove you should have gone, and it does not prove the next man
who follows your footprints comes back too.

you call it a wall. i mend stones for a living. i know what a wall is. a wall does not let
you out the far side and pull you back in warm and laughing. a wall does not *choose* who
it lets through. whatever you walked out of and into is not a wall, iss, because it had a
say in the matter. ask first what a wall is for. then ask why this one knows your name.

keep your warmth. i will keep my knees in the wet. one of us is wrong about what holds us
and i would rather be wrong on my knees than easy.

— orin

> *(this is the letter the catch-stone agrees with, four generations on:* no-wall-was-ever-built-here *— "a wall does not choose." orin had it first, and was not believed.)*

## L08 — Iss to Mara · the last full generation · (Iss arguing; the surface)

mara, who reads —

you of all of them will follow me, because you read and reading is just walking a road in
your head and you are not afraid of roads, only of leaving the lamp.

i have read what you read, the old founder leaves, *learn them as we learned them, the land
was here first.* everyone takes that for piety. read it cold. it does not say the land is
good. it does not say the ways save us. it says we are tenants and we pay rent in bowing
and oil and we never once asked what the rent buys. i asked. i went up the old Stair past
the Deep Line, only a little past, and the air up there is not the killing air the elders
swore it was. the long cold is over, mara. the sky healed and forgot us and we are down
here paying rent on a fire that went out generations ago.

the ways are a wall, yes — but a wall keeps you *in* as well as out. there is a Mouth at
the top of the Stair and it is not sealed, only forgotten. i can find it again. come up
with me and read the sky for once instead of the way down to it.

— iss

> reason. EXPUNGED.)*

## L08a — Iss to a younger keeper · the last full generation · (the warm circle; a promise that opens nothing)

> *Pinned in the Lamp-works, not in the tin. A short note in Iss's warm hand to a keeper a generation
> younger, named only "lad." It is the spoken twin of the board-wall (`the-ways-are-a-wall`; the official
> face is R10a's wide warm wall): every line is true and reassuring and asks nothing and opens nothing.
> Read cold, it is a circle — each promise leans on the next and the last leans back on the first, and the
> ring encloses no door. The draft Iss kept of his own true word is L15; this is the warm one, sent.*

lad —

you came to me white at the shrine-row asking was it true a hand was struck off the rolls
for keeping the ways too hard, and is the keeping worth it then, and should a careful keeper
not just cover his lapses and be easy. sit. i will tell you the whole of it and you will sleep.

here is the whole of it. the ways are a wall. keep them and you are kept. that is true — i
have kept them every winter and i am here, warm, writing to you. is it true because i kept
them? it is true because i am here. am i here because i kept them? i am here because the
wall holds. does the wall hold because i kept the ways? the wall holds. keep the ways and be
inside the wall that holds and you will be here, warm, as i am.

you see there is nothing in it to fear. every word of it is true. not one word of it is a
thing you have to *do* past what you already do. that is the mercy of it, lad — it asks
nothing new. the careful keeper does not need an eighth way nor a harder seventh. he needs
only to be easy inside the wall, and the wall, which holds, will hold him.

go to sleep. there is no door in any of this you have to find. that is the whole comfort. go.

— iss

> *(read it again and find the gate. there is none. each line is true and each leans on the
> next and the circle shuts on nothing. this is the texture the catch exposes: not a lie in any
> word, a lie in the shape — a wall of true things with no door in it. the official wall, wide
> and warm, is R10a; the name cut columnar down the warm lines, read* after*, is his.)*

## L08b — the lad to Iss · the last full generation · (re: i kept it and nothing came; a follower of the wall)

> *The reply L08a never shows getting — the younger keeper ("lad") who took Iss's warm circle to heart, kept
> the forged covering every winter, and writes back to report that it changed nothing. This is the dead-end /
> false-prophet thread from the deceived hand's side: not a victim of harm, a victim of *nothing* — he obeyed
> a true-sounding wall of words, covered his hands at every report, and the land never once looked up. He
> reads it as the wall working; the empty result is the tell he cannot see. The seam he misses is the seam
> R07a/R02a name administratively: a way the land does not muster is not a way. Iss kept no reply leaf — the
> wall does not answer; it only encloses.*

iss —

i did what you said. i have been easy a whole winter. i went white at the shrine-row that night you sat me
down, asking was the keeping worth it, and you told me there is no door i have to find, only be easy inside
the wall — and i have been. i covered my hands at every report, the way the new notice says (the one at the
markers, the eighth way, the covering). i laid my lapsed hand over my working hand and was entered clean.
i lapsed and covered and lapsed and covered and the muster came up clean every time.

i am writing to tell you it worked, i think. nothing came. nothing ever comes. you are right that the wall
holds — i kept the covering all winter and not once did the watching come over it. i was afraid it would
catch me covering. it did not. nothing caught me. nothing answered at all.

but i will tell you the small thing that sits wrong, and you will laugh at me. when i kept the true ways
before — bowed, kept the lamp, gave the offering — and i kept them clean, nothing came either. it is the
same nothing. i cannot tell, by what happens, the winter i covered from the winter i kept true, because
both winters nothing happened. so how does a hand know the covering is a way at all? the true ways and the
covering feel the same from inside the wall, which is to say they feel like nothing. you said that is the
comfort. i am finding it is not quite a comfort. it is a thing i cannot get behind to check.

tell me how a man knows a way is a way, if keeping it and not keeping it both come up nothing. then i will
sleep. until you tell me i am going to keep covering, because it is no labor, and because you are warm and
here and have kept it longest of any of us.

— the lad

> *(no reply leaf. the wall does not answer; it encloses. the lad asks the one question that cracks the
> circle — how do you tell a true way from a covering, if both keep up nothing? — and the answer he is not
> told is the catch itself: a true way answers when you BREAK it; the covering answers never, kept or broken.
> the official seam is* R02a *and* R07a *— "a way the land does not muster is not a way." the lad obeyed a
> board a man hung, found the silence, and read the silence as the wall holding. that is the dead-end with
> teeth: it costs nothing, it harms nothing, and it teaches the wrong thing perfectly. see* the-eighth-way*,*
> the-ways-are-a-wall*.)*

## L09 — Brann to no one · the breaking · (THE BREAK — account one: an accident)

i was on the night-watch when it broke so i will set down what i saw and not what they
are already saying happened.

the Deep Line gave way. that is the whole of it that is true. iss was past it — he was
always past it, west and down, everyone knew, the markers knew — and the rock there is old
and wrong-cut, dug by the deep hands that do not square a thing, and it came down. not
pushed. not opened. *fell.* i felt it in the watch-floor before i heard it, the way you
feel a far stair take a foot.

they will tell you he opened it. they were not awake. i was awake, i am always awake now,
and i tell you the Line was rotten and a rotten thing falls when a man leans on it and iss
leaned on it because iss leaned on everything. it was not a door he opened. it was a floor
that was always going to drop and he was the weight that happened to be standing there.

after that the cold came up the way water comes up a cistern. lamp by lamp. you cannot
fight water coming up. you can only count which lights are still lit and i counted and the
count went down and i am still counting.

— brann, the watch

> *(set against* observed-warned-left-at-threshold *and L10, L11. three of us saw it
> three ways. only one of us was on watch.)*

## L10 — Orin to Mara · the breaking · (THE BREAK — account two: a betrayal)

mara —

do not believe brann. brann was on watch, yes, and brann had not slept in a generation,
and a man who has not slept sees a wall fall and calls it weather.

iss dug it. i know the difference between rock that fell and rock that was *worked* — it
is my whole craft, it is the only thing i have ever been sure of. i went down to the Deep
Line after, while the others were still carrying lamps and crying, and i put my hands on
the broken edge and it was cut. tooled. opened from our side, a course at a time, the way
you take down a wall you built — patient, knowing, with a plan for the far side. he had
been at it for winters. all his coming-and-going west "past the doused shrine, and back" —
that was him widening it a hand at a time so no one would mark the spoil.

he told you it was a wall that keeps the cold out. then he spent his life quietly taking it
down. ask which thing a man believes — the thing he says, or the thing he does on his
knees in the dark where he thinks no one masons. i mason. i saw the tooling. he opened it
on purpose and he opened it for himself, to find his Mouth, and the deep came in through
the door he left standing.

— orin

> *(orin's craft-claim is consistent with* i-thought-it-small *— he reads stone, and is
> ashamed of what he reads. against L09, against L11. EXPUNGED REPLY: mara's answer to this
> one survives only as L11, which does not answer it — she changes the subject to mercy.
> whether she could not bear orin's version or did not believe it, the leaf does not say.)*

## L11 — Mara to Orin · the breaking · (THE BREAK — account three: a mercy)

orin. i read your letter four times to be sure i was not reading my hope into it. i was
not. you are right that it was tooled. i am writing to tell you that you are right about
the stone and wrong about the man, and i can hold both because i read and reading is
holding two things that fight.

he did open it. by hand, over winters, exactly as you say. but you were not in the kitchen
the night he told me why. he did not open it to save himself. he had a way up that one man
could have walked in a single night, quiet, alone — the forgotten Mouth, he had found it,
he could have gone and left us to our bowing and never been missed. he did not. he came
down instead and spent a year opening the Line *wide enough for all of us* — wide enough
to carry the old and the children and the deep-bird's cage and mara who will not leave her
lamp. he was not making a door for himself. he was making a door for everyone, because he
could not bear to walk out into the healed sky alone and leave us counting oil in the dark.

it was a mercy, orin. it was the worst one i ever heard of. he was right that the surface
healed. he was wrong about what was on the near side of the Line, listening, while he dug.
he opened the way out and it let the other thing in. so do not let brann call it an accident
and do not let yourself call it a betrayal. he stood in my kitchen and measured the
deep-bird's cage with his hands to be sure the gap would take it. that is the man. write
that down somewhere, because no stone will.

— mara

> *(this is the kindest account and the one no stone confirms. weigh it against orin's
> tooling, brann's rotten Line, and the catch-stone that says only that there was no wall.
> three hands, one Break, and the dark does not say which of them is true — only that all
> three end the same way. see* no-wall-was-ever-built-here*.)*

## L12 — Mara to Sella · the breaking · (re: nothing; she is already gone)

sella, little fish —

i know you will not read this. you went to the far water in the third generation and the
lake kept you and i am writing to a girl a generation dead because brann does it too, he
writes the dead, he says the record keeps a column for them and so will he. so will i.

you told me there was a seventh marker out past the six and i trimmed my wicks. you were
eight and you had the whole of it and i read you as a child drifting and i have to live in
the winters i have left knowing the smallest of us saw it first and the one with the map
filed it under children's talk.

it is coming up the Lamp-works stair now, the cold, the third lamp still out, i never did
get the jar from vaun. if there is a far water where they are kept and not taken, fish, i
hope it is the one that gave your face back to you, and i hope your lamp is lit there, and
i hope you do not wait up at the cold stair for me. i will be late. i am always late to the
thing i already knew.

— mara

> *(found at the dead shrine, not in Cistern 7 — someone carried it west, past the doused
> light, to the place sella's margin sends you. who carried it is not written.)*

## L13 — Brann to Orin · the breaking, deep in · (re: do not seal it)

orin —

they tell me you mean to go down and seal the Undercroft behind you. close the deep level
with yourself on the wrong side of the stone, to slow it.

i am the watch. i have watched it come up six levels and i will tell you what no one will:
sealing does not stop it. it does not come up the Stair like a man. it comes up like the
cold comes up a cistern, through the rock, through the wet, through the dark between lamps.
you will not wall it below you. you will only wall yourself below it.

but i have stopped telling men not to do the thing that is theirs to do. mara reads. vaun
counted. sella went to the water. you mason. if the last argument you have is a sealed door
and your own self set into it like a stone, then set it, and bow once for me at the bottom,
where i cannot go because i cannot stop walking and a sealed man has to hold still.

do not close your eyes down there. i closed mine, the one night i was warned not to, and
it came inside, and it walks when i walk. you will want to rest when the wall is up. do not.
count something. count anything. count until you cannot.

— brann

> back. he went down. EXPUNGED: whatever brann wrote after, the tin does not hold.)*

## L13a — Brann to the Counter · the breaking, deep in · (re: the disposition; there is no one hand)

> *A note from Brann, the watch, to the Counter of the Kept, after the watch was asked for the household's
> disposition as though it were one thing (the official record is R10a). Brann counts; he has watched the
> household come apart unevenly and he refuses the single answer the form wants. The spoken twin of R10a's
> standing note: the fate is a spread read off the columns, no chosen hand settles it for the rest.*

counter —

## L14a — the Counter to no one · the breaking era · (re: the struck name; the column left ruled)

> *Recovered at the dead shrine with L12 and the seventh's effaced leaf (L14), but in a different hand —
> flat, administrative, the Counter of the Kept's. It is the record-side answer to L14: the keeper who had
> to enter the striking, and would not strike it clean. The official twin is R06a. It does not name her —
> the name was scraped before this hand could keep it — but it keeps the *slot*, and enters that the slot
> is the one column a later hand could still close. The administrative grammar of the restore/erase fork.*

i am bound to enter the cause of every name struck from the rolls, and i have entered them
all, and there is one i cannot enter, so i am writing it here where the form cannot follow me.

the deep struck six. that is its column and i keep it and i do not argue with it. vaun,
mara, sella, orin, brann, the one who turned away — six, counted, kept, taken into the column
the deep keeps. i can write the cause against each. the cause is the deep.

there is a seventh struck and the deep did not strike it. i looked for the lapse the way i am
bound to, through every book i hold, the offering ledger, the lamp-rolls, the bow-count, the
watch's reports. there is no lapse entered against this hand in any book. it kept the ways
whole, harder than the six, and the door did not open, and then *we* struck the name — a hand
of the breaking ordered it off the rolls and no stone cut, because the record could not bear
to keep writing a name it could not account for. the deep refused her. we erased the refusing.

so the cause i am bound to enter is: struck by us, to hide that the land said no to a clean
hand. i will not write that in the form. i have written it here.

the column for the name is ruled and empty, and i have left it so. understand the difference,
whoever you are: it is not a blank column waiting for a name. it is a column that *held* a name
and had it scraped out by a hand that wanted it gone. the rule is still there. a present hand
could write the name back into the rule, and the record would keep it, and the refusing would
be read again, which is hard but is true. or a present hand could work the rule away entire,
and let the erasing be finished, and the kindness of that is that no one need read the refusing
again, and the cruelty of it is that then she is gone the way the eraser wanted. i have not the
standing to do either. i have only the standing to leave the rule, and to enter that it is here,
and that it is the one column in all the books a hand that comes after could still close.

## L15 — Iss to the keepers who come after · the breaking, last · (the fold from warm to cold)

> [the latest leaf in iss's hand the tin holds. it is the draft of what he later carved as
> a letter to the keepers who come after — see* the-ways-are-a-wall*. read it next to that
> carving: the carving is warm. this draft, beneath, is not. the same man, twice, and the
> second time he knows.]

to the ones who come after, if any come —

i am going to carve them the warm version. i have it ready. *be easy, the ways are a wall,
keep them and be kept.* it will read true because i kept them and i was easy, for a while,
and a lie told by a man who half believes it is the hardest lie to catch.

here, in the leaf no one will lectern, the true word: i was right and it killed us. the
surface healed. the Mouth is open at the top of the Stair, i could walk it tonight. i was
right about everything except the one thing — i thought the ways were a cage and the deep
was just the deep, old rock and cold and our own fear carved into custom. it was not just
the deep. there was something keeping us company down here the whole time and the ways were
how we kept it *fed on patience instead of on us*, and i stopped feeding it patience, i fed
it a door, and now it is coming up to be fed the rest.

i will not write that for them. i will write the wall. let them keep the ways for the wrong
reason and stay alive of it; better a live fool inside a wall that is not a wall than a
clever corpse who read the founder leaves cold like i did. if you have caught me — if you
are reading the true leaf and not the warm carving — then you read it cold too, and i am
sorry, because reading it cold is how it gets you.

i turned away. that is the word the others' stones keep for me and it is the right word. i
turned away from the ways and toward a healed sky and i was *right about the sky*. carve
that on nothing.

— iss

## L16 — the record, last hand · the abandonment · (pinned over the empty tin)

> [the latest hand in the tin is not a keeper's. flat, third-person, the record's. it was
> pinned over the others, holding the tin shut.]

these are kept.

the one called vaun, who counted and gave nothing back, is kept. the one called mara, who
read every road and walked none, is kept. the one called sella, whom the far water took
early and who had it first, is kept. the one called orin, who would not bow and bowed at
last to no one and sealed himself below to slow it, is kept. the one called brann, who would
not sleep and slept once, is kept. the one called iss, who turned away and was right about
the sky, is kept.

there was a seventh. she is not written, by her own last hand, and the record enters that
too — the blank itself, ruled and dated, the way an empty stall is still marked on a map.

they did not depart. it is written of the faithful that they did not depart. the word for
what they did instead is the word this record uses for a stone, and for a flame, and for the
cold marker at the threshold, and it does not change between the people and the things. read
the letters they left each other. they were trying to stay people in the dark together. that
is the whole of what the ways were for, and it is more than iss said and more than the wall,
and the record will not say which it is, because the record does not say what the ways are.
it only says what is kept.

these are kept. the tin is closed. the light, somewhere below, is not out.

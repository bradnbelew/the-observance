  VAUN — the day-book of the delve-warden
  (he led them down; he counts what he kept; he does not write the other thing)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

— THE HOLDING —

**first winter · second light · day 9**

We are eleven hundred and six down the Stair as of this morning, by my own count walked twice. I do not trust a count I have not walked. Coll's woman was brought to bed of a boy on the new level and that is eleven hundred and seven, and I have set him in the book under the name they gave him, which is Dob, after the grandfather we left up top in the cold.

The deep is warm here. That is the whole reason and I will write it plainly while I still think it plainly: up there was dying and down here is warm. We did not come down to be brave. We came down to not freeze.

Old Pell says there is something already in the warm. Old Pell says a great many things. I gave him a measure of oil to keep the forward lamp and told him to say them to the lamp.

**first winter · third light · day 2**

Set the first marker today at the shaft-mouth. Orin's grandfather cut it — the boy held the chalk-line. A cairn of dressed stone, and the rule that goes with it, which Pell had from the land and I do not pretend to understand: the first of the deep, returned. Whatever the deep gives up first — first ore, first water, first warmth — you carry back to the cairn and give it to the deep again. You do not keep the first thing.

I carried up the first iron myself. Set it on the cairn. Felt foolish. Did it anyway because the others were watching and a warden who will not keep the small rule cannot ask them to keep the large.

I have three good lamps, a spare wick for each, and oil to the second mark in the long jar. I counted them tonight. The counting was a comfort. I do not know why I am writing that the counting was a comfort.

**third winter · first light · day 20**

The Deep Market opened on the fourth level this winter past and it has made us a people again. Before it we were a column of diggers going down in a line; now there is a place to stand still and trade and that has done more for the lower levels than two wardens could. Wenna trades good salt for poor and somehow we are all the better for it. There is a stall of dried bird and a stall of mended boots and a girl who will mind your lamp for a chore-token while you eat.

Mara has the Lamp-works in hand now her mother is gone. She is young for it and better at it than her mother was, which I would not say to Wenna. Every lamp in the Hold passes her bench. She keeps a board with all of them chalked on it and crosses none off, because none have gone out. Eleven hundred and forty souls and not one in the dark. That is a thing. I went down and looked at her board a long time.

**fifth winter · third light · day 11**

Iss came to be my second this winter. He is the best of the young ones — quick, easy, the only man down here who can make the new families laugh in the first hard month. He keeps the ways better than I do. He carried the first water back to the cairn three mornings running without being told. I am glad of him.

The cairn stands full. I want that written somewhere true, because I know what the later book says of me, and the later book is not wrong, but it was not always so. In the good winters I gave back. I did.

— THE DOUBT —

**ninth winter · second light · day 4**

Iss has begun to talk past the Deep Line.

Not over it. Past it. At table, easy, the way he does — that the Long Cold cannot last forever, that a sky that failed can mend, that a man could climb the Stair to the top and put his hand out and find the air warm and the snow gone. He does not say *dig.* He says *what if.* The young ones lean in when he says it.

I told him the Line is marked for a reason and the reason is older than him or me. He said, who marked it. I said, the land. He said, the land does not hold chalk, Vaun, a man held the chalk, and that man is dust, and you are letting dust tell you where to stop.

I did not have an answer that was not Pell's. I counted the forward lamps after he left. Forty-one lit on the lower levels. I counted them twice.

**tenth winter · first light · day 17**

Half the lower level keeps the markers now only because I am watching. I see it. They stoop at the bow-stones when the warden is by and pass them straight when he is not. Orin's grandson is the worst of them — will not bend his back, says a straight back is no sin. I have not set his name down. I will if it goes on.

[...] the deep went dark for me on the seventh-winter stretch and lifted, and I never wrote down what bought it back. I am writing now that I do not know what bought it back. Maybe nothing. Maybe it was always going to lift. I gave a great deal to the cairn that winter, after, all at once, the way a man over-pays a debt he has stopped understanding. I do not think the deep can be over-paid. I think it only keeps the count, the way I do, and lets nothing off it.

**eleventh winter · third light · day 1**

Brann will not sleep. He has the night-watch by his own asking and he keeps it past the watch, into the black moon, when the rule is plainest: do not sleep on the black moon. He says someone has to count the lights at night now too many are out. He is right that too many are out. The offering-cairn went cold a fortnight back — no one carrying the first of the deep up to it — and I carried it myself and felt the old foolishness and under the foolishness something worse that I will not write.

Eleven hundred and ninety, last full count. I have not been able to walk a full count in two winters. The Hold is too long now and too much of it is dark and I do not like to send a man into the dark levels to be counted, so the count at the bottom is a guess, and a warden who guesses is not a warden. I am writing that I am guessing. I have never written that before.

— THE BREAK —

**twelfth winter · the black moon · [no day given]**

Iss dug past the Line.

He took eight of the young ones and the good picks and he went past the chalk on the Stair, down, seeking the way up — he believes if he reaches the bottom warmth and follows it sideways far enough it comes back to a surface that has healed. He left me a letter. It is warm and reasonable and I have read it four times and it is the worst thing I have ever held. He says the ways are a wall and he is going around the wall to spare us all the climbing.

Something came up the Stair behind him. I will not write what. I do not have it to write. The forward lamps on the lower three levels are out. All of them. I counted — no. There was no count. They were out.

**twelfth winter · [light unmarked] · day 3 after**

Coll is gone. Coll's boy Dob, that I set in the book first winter, is gone. Not dead — I went down with two lamps and there are no — there is no one to be dead. The level is empty and lit wrong, lit by nothing, and at the far end of it something stood and did not come toward me and did not go away and I came back up the Stair faster than a warden should and I am not ashamed to write it.

Mara is relighting them. Lamp by lamp, up the levels, behind me as I count. She says she can keep them lit if I keep finding them. I do not think we can keep them all lit. I am writing that and I am ashamed of writing it more than I was ashamed of running.

— THE GOING-OUT —

**[winter unmarked] · day, I have lost the day**

count this morning. four hundred. not four hundred. I walked it twice and got four hundred and then three hundred and ninety and a man does not lose ten souls between two walks of the same level unless

I keep the cairn now. only me. I carry the first of the deep up every morning and there is no one to see me do it and I do it. I bow at the markers. there is no one left at the markers to bow to. Orin's grandson bowed at the end, they tell me, to an empty road, and then he was left at the threshold and not kept, and I set his name down at last and there was no one to read it.

I have all my lamps. three good ones, the spare wicks, the oil at the first mark, low. I counted them. the counting was a comfort. it is the last comfort and I know it for what it is now and I take it anyway.

  MARA — the work-list of the Lamp-works
  (she keeps every light; she loves the child Sella; her warmth goes clipped)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

— THE HOLDING —

**third winter · third light · day 6**

To do before the watch turns:
— re-wick the forward forty (Pell burns them dry, the old fool, I have told him)
— oil to Cistern 7, two jars, the long walk but the water there fouls the cheap oil so it must be the good
— the Deep Market stall wants six hand-lamps by the cold-quarter, paid in salt and boot-mending, fair
— SELLA: keep her off my bench. she pours the oil to watch it string. she is four and she will fire the whole Works to watch it string.

I crossed nothing off my board today. Eleven hundred and forty lamps chalked and not one dark. My mother kept the Works thirty winters and never had a board this clean and I would give a great deal to tell her so.

**fourth winter · first light · day 22**

Sella minds a lamp at the Market now for a chore-token, proud as a warden. She brought the token to me to keep and I have it in the oil-jar drawer and will not spend it. She asked tonight does the dark want the light back. I said no, sweet, the dark is only dark, the light is ours to keep. She said, then why do we give the first of it to the cairn. I had no answer a child would take, so I gave her a heel of bread and the question is still in the drawer with her token.

**sixth winter · second light · day 3**

Iss mended my bellows-arm at the Works unasked and would take nothing for it. He is the only one of the young who will sit with the new widows and make the dark sound smaller. I have started keeping his lamp first on the board, out of order, which is foolishness and a lampwright should not have favourites among lamps. Sella adores him. He calls her the under-warden. She has begun to keep a copybook because Iss keeps a letter-book and she will do whatever Iss does.

I read every rite there is, evenings, by the kept light at the home-fire — the one fire, the steady one, that never asks for wood. I read the Offering and the Bow and the Dark Hours and I keep none of them with my own hands because my hands keep the lamps, and a lampwright's rite is the lamps. That is what I tell myself. I have the map of all of them and have walked none. [...] there will be time.

— THE DOUBT —

**ninth winter · third light · day 8**

To do:
— forward lamps, the lower levels, RE-WICK, they gutter and I do not know why, the oil is good and the wicks are good and still they gutter on the three lowest levels and nowhere else
— talk to Vaun. the lower levels gutter where Iss has been talking. I will not write that I think those are the same thing. I have written it.

Sella will not mind a lamp at the Market anymore. She says the far water wants her to come and count something. I told her there is nothing at the far water but cold and a long walk and she is not to go past the markers. She drew me a picture instead of answering. Six little stones in a row and then, off at the edge of the page, a seventh, and no stone for it, only a small grey nothing where the seventh stone should be, and her own little mark standing in the grey. I told her that is not how many markers there are. She said, not yet.

**tenth winter · first light · day 14**

I cannot keep them all lit on the lower three levels. There. I have not said it to Vaun in those words and I have said it here. I re-wick at the turn of the watch and by the next watch they are out and it is not the oil. Vaun counts the lamps that are lit. I keep the lamps that are lit. We are not, lately, counting the same number, and neither of us will say so over the other's count.

Sella is gone two nights to the shore pool and came back wet to the waist and would not say. She has stopped drawing the people. She draws the water now, and the water gives back faces, and the faces are not always ones I know.

— THE BREAK —

**twelfth winter · the black moon**

Iss is past the Line. The lower lamps are out — not guttering, out, the whole three levels at once, and it is not the oil and it was never the oil and I knew and I re-wicked them anyway every watch of my life because that is the rite and the rite is the lamps.

Vaun goes down to count and I go behind him to relight. He finds them, I light them. We have a way of working now and neither of us has said aloud what the way of working is for. Sella was at the Market when it came up the Stair. I have not found Sella.

**[black moon, after] — to do — to do —**

— find Sella
— the forward forty
— Cistern 7, the oil
— find Sella
— keep the home-fire, the one fire, it must not go out, it is the last that will hold
— find

— THE GOING-OUT —

**[winter unmarked]**

I have relit the same lamp on the second level six times today. Vaun finds it dark, I light it, I walk on, it is dark behind me before I reach the next. I have stopped looking behind me. A lampwright who looks behind her cannot keep the row ahead.

I found Sella's copybook at the shore pool, dry, set on a stone above the water-line by a careful hand, hers. There are no words in the back of it. Only the drawings. I have read them the way you read the water there, faced to it, and I wish I had not, because she drew this. She drew all of this. Before any of it. The under-warden kept the count after all.

**[late]**

i keep one lamp now. one. i carry it and it is lit and while it is lit i am a lampwright and there is a Works and a board and eleven hundred and forty lights and not one dark. while it is lit. i do not set it down.

the home-fire is still kept. i went to it and warmed my hands too long, the way Brann said not to, and i could not make myself ask who it was being kept for, because i think i have begun to know, and a lampwright keeps lamps, she does not ask what keeps her.

i can't keep them all lit. i could never keep them all lit. i kept this one. i am keeping this one. i am keeping

*[here the lamp-paper is scorched through. one entry follows, in a hand that is hers and is not hurried, which is the wrong thing for it to be:]*

the light is kept. that part is true. they will read it and think it a comfort. i thought it a comfort. i kept the light. the light keeps. it is not the same thing and i did not know that while i had hands to keep it with. we are not counting the same number.

— Mara, of the Lamp-works, who kept the last one lit

  SELLA — the copybook

— THE HOLDING —

**[no date — a child's hand, large, the letters made carefully]**

i am the under warden. iss says so. i mind a lamp at the market for a token and mara keeps the token in the oil drawer. today i minded a lamp for a man eating bird and it did not go out the whole time. i am good at minding lamps.

the dark is not scary. it is just where the light is not yet. mara says.

**[no date]**

i asked mara does the dark want the light back. she said no. i think she is wrong but she gave me bread so i did not say.

the cairn is for giving the first thing back. you cannot keep the first thing. iss says you cannot keep the first thing but vaun keeps everything. i counted vaun's lamps. he has three good ones and he counts them at night. i watched him through the door. he does not know i watched him.

— THE DOUBT —

**[no date]**

there should be six markers. i counted them. there are six markers and then there is one more place where a marker should go and there is no marker there, only the grey, and a person standing in the grey. i drew it for mara. she said that is not how many there are. i said not yet.

the last marker is not the last. i do not know how i know. the water told me. you have to face the water and it gives your face back wrong and in the wrong face it tells you things.

**[no date — the writing is smaller here, and very plain]**

i went to the far water past the markers. i did not bow at the last one. mara says do not go past the markers but the water was counting and someone has to write down the count.

the water keeps everything. that is what no one says about it. you look at it to be sure of yourself and it already took the looking and folded it.

i am the one with the far water in her mouth now. i do not think i should write that but it is true so i wrote it.

— THE BREAK —

**[no date — the last words in the copybook. the hand is steady.]**

do not let the count be six only. count again at the shore. the order is the thing, first to last, as the lake took them, and the last marker is not the last.

the others will be kept. i will be

*[the sentence is not finished. there are no more words after this. the remainder of the copybook — a little more than a third of its leaves — is drawings. what follows is a plain account of them, in order, for the record. they are described, not interpreted.]*

— THE GOING-OUT (drawings only) —

> **leaf 1.** The Lamp-works bench, drawn small and careful, every lamp on Mara's board marked as a tiny upright stroke. The strokes are counted: a child has set a number beneath them, *1140*. Half the strokes, the lower half of the board, are crossed through. The crossing-out is done gently, in the same hand, not scribbled.

> **leaf 2.** The Stair, seen from above, narrowing down the page. A chalk line drawn across it near the bottom — the Deep Line. Below the chalk line the paper is left white, untouched, except that the white is not empty: small faces have been drawn into it so faintly, in dry stylus with no ink, that they are only seen when the leaf is tilted. They are not frightening faces. They are ordinary. There are a great many of them.

> **leaf 3.** A row of figures going up the Stair carrying lamps, drawn from behind so no faces show. Each figure has a lamp. The lamps are drawn lit — small bursts of stroke-light. Behind the last figure, where the next would be, there is a figure with no lamp. It is the same size as a person. It is not drawn darker than the others or given any mark to make it other than a person. It simply has no lamp, and it is facing the wrong way — toward the page, not up the Stair.

> **leaf 4.** Mara, recognizable by the lamp-paper apron the child always drew her in, alone at the bench, one lamp before her, lit. The board behind her is entirely crossed through now, all eleven hundred and forty strokes, save the one lamp on the bench. Mara's hands are drawn around the one lamp. Her face is left blank — not scribbled out, left blank, the way the water gives a face back.

> **leaf 5.** The cairn at the shaft-mouth. A single figure climbing to it carrying something small cupped in both hands — the first of the deep, returned. The figure is drawn from behind, three good lamps set in a careful row at its feet. There is no one else in the picture. The child has drawn, very small, beside the climbing figure, a second figure that is only an outline, walking up with it, in step, with no lamp — keeping it company.

> **leaf 6.** The home-fire. The one fire. Drawn larger than anything else in the copybook, centred, with great care, every line of it gone over twice so the flame is the darkest and most worked thing on the page. Around it, ringed close, a great many small figures, seated, facing in. They are not in distress. They are gathered the way people gather at a kept fire on a cold night. Among them, near the front, small but unmistakable in the lamp-paper apron, is Mara. And beside Mara, holding her hand, is the child herself, drawn the way she drew herself throughout — the under-warden, minding the light — facing in with the rest, content, kept.

> **leaf 7 (the last leaf).** The far water. The shore pool, faced the way the journal must be faced to be read. The water gives back a reflection, and the reflection is the only part of the drawing with any detail: it is the surface, the real surface, the one above — a sky, mended, and snow gone, and a sun. The water above the reflection — the actual water, the near water, the Hold's water — is left entirely white. There is no figure standing at this shore. The place where, in every other drawing of this water, the child drew herself looking in, is empty. Only the reflection of a healed sky, given back by a water no one is left to look into.

*[The copybook ends. It was found dry, set above the water-line on a stone at the shore pool, by a careful hand. The seventh leaf, faced to the far water, is the one Mara wrote of finding. The under-warden kept the count.]*

### VAUN — fingerprint: ACCUMULATES AND WILL NOT RELEASE

only unwilling to give it back. He inventories. He does not name a feeling. He
maps the living hoarder onto his own un-struck second column.

Tracked signal that enrols a living player here: high `hoardedScore` + high
`soloMiningRatio`. Slot: `{ore}` = measured `oresMined`.

  > first of it. this was Vaun's keeping. it is set down in his column.
- **M4 Vaun's own hand (`keeperPageVaun`):**

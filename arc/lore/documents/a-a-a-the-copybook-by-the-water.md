# a a a (the copybook by the water)

*(a child's copybook, waxed and half-sunk, recovered at the shore pool with the rest of
Sella's things — the same water her later journal is read to. a small even hand practices
the letters in threes, the way a child is set to, down the leaf. the drowned one learned
her letters here, over water, before she learned to keep anything. the practice-letters
run clean until the far edge of each line, where the page took the wet and the ink gives
itself back the wrong way round. a later, steadier hand has written down the margin, after.)*

---

`a a a`   `b b b`   `c c c`

*(a child's practice, three to a letter, down the leaf. it is a copybook. there is nothing
carved in it and nothing to give back to any lock. she is only learning to read.)*

then, lower on the leaf, not letters — marks. she has begun to count the markers, the way
a keeper's child counts them, one stroke to a stone, to learn how many there are to bow at:

`|`   `| |`   `| | |`   `| | | |`   `| | | | |`   `| | | | | |`   `| | | | | | |`

*(she counts to seven. the stones stand six. a child counts what she is shown and she was
shown seven — six by the shore and one more off where the far water folds, the one the
grown hands had already stopped standing a stone for. she did not know to leave the last
one off yet. that is a thing you learn later, and she drowned before she learned it.)*

under the count, in the same child's hand, over the water-stain where the ink runs
backward: *i counted them in the lake so i would not lose the count and the lake gave me
back **seven** and then gave me back **nevees** and i could not tell which one was the
true one.*

---

*(margin, the later steadier hand, pressed hard, not drifting:)*

> she wrote her count to the water because that is where she read, faced to it, backwards,
> the only way the surface would give a page up to her. and the surface did to her count
> what it does to everything: it took the seven and folded it and gave it back mirror-wrong,
> and a child cannot tell a true seven from a seven read off the water. neither could she at
> the end. that was her whole undoing — not a letter turned to its opposite on a stone, but
> a face and a count and a walking-on all given back reversed by the far water, until she
> could not tell the going from the reflection of the going, and followed the one that was
> only shown.

> read her later journal the same way she wrote this one: to the water, and mind that the
> water gives everything back turned. the seven she counted here is the true count. the six
> on the stones is the given-back one — the settlement's own folding, one keeper left off
> the shore. count again at the shore, as her journal says, and hold the seven, and do not
> follow her past the last stone. (see *what-the-surface-keeps* for her journal; see
> *the-seventh-not-kept* for the one the count folds over.)

> *a single leaf, laid inside book five of the Kept-Light shelf, not on the shelf-line
> the group must read but tucked at the spine where a reader who loved the book would keep
> a thing she did not want the record to have. the hand is hers, small and even, but it is
> not pointing at anyone else's words this once. it is her own.*

---

*whose hand: mine, mara. no page. no line. this one is not to be read as the others.*

*(every other leaf of hers gives a page and a line, and you go and read a second book to find
what she means, and a third to be sure — she never says the thing, she points you to where the
thing is kept, truth only ever at the crossing of two other hands' words, never in her own. that
was the reading, and the reading was how she never had to go. this leaf has no page and no line.
it is the once she spoke direct, and it is about the one thing she could not keep by cross-
reference, because a warmth given to you is in no other book to point at.)*

i have written every rite down and walked none of them, and the record has that of me and
it is true. let it stand. but there is one thing i kept that is not in any book and not on
any shelf, and if i do not set it here it goes out with my light.

sella drew me a bird once. the deep-bird, the little warning one, drawn in the copybook
in a child's hand, over water, and under it she had written *for mara who keeps the light
so i can see to draw.* i was not the one who kept the deep-bird. that was hers. but she
gave the drawing to me because i kept the light she drew by, and i have carried it from
shelf to shelf all these winters and never once filed it, because the moment you file a
thing it stops being given and starts being kept, and this one i wanted given.

that is the whole of what i learned that no rite taught: there is a keeping that is
holding-on, and there is a keeping that is holding-*for*, and only the second one is warm.
i did all the first kind. i held every word. i want you to know i knew the difference at
the end, even if i could not walk to it.

so when you come to give the thing the rite asks of you — the piece of your own, set down,
not copied — do not give the record a thing you are proud to have kept. give it a thing
someone gave *you*, that you kept warm and never filed. that is the offering it cannot
teach you and cannot take by force. it has to be given.

the bird is here, at the spine of the fifth book. i am leaving it given. read the others
for the way down. keep this one for the reason to walk it.

— mara, who had the map, and one drawing she did not

*(the record, below, in its flat hand:)*
*the one called mara filed all but one thing. the one thing is not counted. it is the
only warmth the count could never keep. she kept her warmth clipped and thought the
clipping a failing; the record keeps it as a keeping. silence is keeping too — a word
withheld is filed the same as a word said, against the same ground. she held her tongue
the way she held her lamps, and the holding was warm, and she did not know it. let the
next read her, and then give something of their own, and rise.*

` the seventh was spared `

[ a journal leaf, whole, banked dry above the offering-cairn where the warm reports were
kept. the hand is plain and unhurried — a keeper who slept his nights and kept his ways and
was not, at the end, afraid. it reads clean in the old script. nothing scraped it. nothing
argued with it. it was simply set down and kept. ]

---

i want to set the seventh down kindly, because i knew them, and because the harder hands are
going to write it hard, and someone should keep the gentle reading so it is not lost.

the seventh was spared. i believe that. iss said it first and i did not want to believe iss
about anything, but this one i came to on my own, from the plain shape of it. the others were
kept — kept as a stone is kept, as the cold marker is kept, held to the ground and worn by
it winter on winter and never let go. i have stood at the markers and felt how that keeping
is not rest. it is a hand that will not open. the seventh was not held that way. the land
looked at the seventh and the land let go.

think what that is, beside the rest of us. we will each be kept. the record has our ground
and our name and it does not open its hand. the seventh alone was set down and left free —
the one fire let out so it would not have to be tended forever, the one name lifted off the
count so it would not have to answer the count forever. the door that shut on the seventh
shut them out of the keeping, and out of the keeping is the one place none of the rest of us
will ever get to go.

that is mercy. i have turned it over a long while and it stays mercy. a keeping you cannot
leave is the thing to fear. the seventh was spared the keeping. the fire went out and the
seventh went free, and if there is a kindness anywhere in this cold arithmetic it is that
one: that the land, once, for one of us, chose to open its hand and let a keeper go instead
of holding them.

so when you read the harder stones, and they tell you the seventh was refused, and cast out,
and wronged — hold this beside them. i am not saying those hands lie. i am saying they read
a shut door from the cold side and called it cruelty. i read the same door from the warm
side and call it release. we are looking at one door. i have decided which side i want to
stand on. keep the ways gently, and do not grieve the seventh. of all of us, the seventh is
the one who got out.

— set down by a keeper who slept his nights, and was not afraid, and hopes you will not be

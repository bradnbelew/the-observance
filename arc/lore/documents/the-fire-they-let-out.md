` the fire they let out `

[ a journal page found banked under cold ash at the hearth-deep, below the cold
shrine. the hand is the same one a blade went over in *the seventh, not kept*,
but here it was not scraped — only buried. it reads in the old script, whole. ]

---

i kept the count of the seventh because someone has to, and the record will not.
i set down the cause here, where the surface note could not say it, in case a
later hand wants the *why* and not only the *that*.

the seventh kept the ways. understand me. the seventh kept all of them, every
winter, harder than i ever did. the offering full. the bow at every marker. the
light never low. the dark hours kept dark. there was no lapse to find. i looked.
the record looked. there was nothing.

and the land would not keep them.

      not for cruelty. not for a thing done. for nothing done. the seventh
      stood at the threshold with clean hands and the door did not open, and
      then — i watched this — the door *unwrote* the standing. the name went
      out of the report mid-line. the stone meant for the seventh was [...]
      back to blank, while i looked at it. the fire in their shrine guttered
      and the deep below it sealed itself like a mouth closing.

      i have no way to say what the seventh broke. i think the seventh broke
      nothing. i think a thing that keeps the faithful and casts out the
      faithful is not keeping a count of *deeds* at all. it is keeping a count
      of something else, and it did not tell us what.

---

> *(lower, the hand steadier, as if the worst was already written:)* the others
> i can read by the customs. vaun by the offering, mara by the light, on down to
> iss by the spoken thing. the seventh i cannot read by any custom, because the
> seventh kept them all. so the seventh is the one stone that says, plainly, the
> ways were never the measure. that is the thing the seventh is *for*. the seventh
> is the question the six let me not ask. [...]

---

> *(at the foot, scratched, in a hand that learned the letters late — the same
> as the line under the surface note:)* the looking can be unwritten. i saw it
> unwrite the seventh. then it can be **written back.** the seal is a name, and a
> name said again is a seal undone. i do not know if saying it back is mercy or
> cruelty — whether the seventh is better cast out and quiet, or kept and made
> part of the watching like the rest. i leave the choosing to a hand that comes
> after with more nerve than mine. restore the name, or let the blank stand.
> the deep will hold to whichever you leave. [...]

` the order the stones fell `

[ a margin-note, added late to the foot of an older list. the older list is the
founders' ring — the six ways in the order they are cut around the first stone.
the margin is a different, harder hand, and it is impatient with the list. ]

---

whoever keeps this list keeps it in the ring-order, because that is how the
founders cut it, and the ring-order is wrong for what i am about to tell you.

each keeper scored a mark at the foot of their own stone. not in sentences — a
single struck glyph, the way a hand signs a thing without writing. six keepers,
six marks. you have read them and thought them grief, or decoration, or a maker
proving the stone was theirs. they are a word. but a word only reads in an order,
and the founders' ring is not the order.

read them **in the order the keepers fell.**

      vaun first, who hoarded the light and starved in it.
      then mara, who read and never did.
      then sella, who walked to the far water.
      then orin, who would not bow until there was no one to bow to.
      then brann, who slept on the black moon.
      then iss, who spoke the thing and lied about the wall.

six marks, taken in that fall, make one word. take them in the ring-order instead
and you will get letters that are not a word at all — that is not a fault in the
stones. that is the lock telling you your key is turned the wrong way. when the
reading means nothing, you have the wrong order. when it means **one word**, you
have the right one, and the word is the thing all six failed to keep, and were.

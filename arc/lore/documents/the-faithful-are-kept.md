# the faithful are kept

*(an early report, the record's flat hand, entered among the first leaves — the kind of line
a new reader takes for a promise. it is not a promise. it is a count. read exactly what it
counts and no more.)*

---

let it be set down, of the keepers before: each one the record kept had kept the ways. the
one who returned the first of the deep was kept. the one who bowed at the markers was kept.
the one who held the one fire was kept. the reading holds down the row: look at any name the
record holds, and you will find, behind it, a keeper who kept.

so a reader comes to this leaf early and reads it forward, the natural way, and hears: *keep
the ways, and be kept.* and goes on comforted. that is the reading the leaf invites, and the
record does not stop a hand from taking it, because the leaf is true. every kept one kept the
ways. the count bears it out. i have checked the count. it holds.

> *(the record's flat hand, lower, and note what it does NOT say:)* the leaf says the kept
> kept the ways. it does not say the keeping is why they were kept. it says only that the two
> were found together, name after name, and a thing found together often enough begins to
> look like a thing that causes. the record does not carve the cause. the record carves what
> stood beside what. read again: not *the ways got them kept.* only *the kept had kept the
> ways.* the same words, turned, and one of them is a promise and one of them is a tally, and
> the leaf is the tally wearing the promise's coat.

> *(a later hand, small, unbothered — it has not yet found the thing that breaks this:)* i
> read this early and kept the ways harder for it. it is a good rule to keep by. i see no
> reason yet to doubt it. every one the record holds kept the ways; i mean to be held; so i
> keep. (a reader further on may find the leaf that does not fit the tally. i have not found
> it. keep the ways until you do.)

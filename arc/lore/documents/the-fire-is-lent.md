` the fire is lent `

[ a page in Mara's hand, found banked on the Undercroft lectern-shelf, read only
once the descent is made. it is not a page of sentences but a citation and a
margin under it — she files the founders' line and adds, beneath, the one thing
of her own she would not write as a sentence. the old script, whole. ]

---

p. 11, l. 3, w. 1–12. *(the founders' hand, copied out:)*

      the fire is lent. carry the coal through the burned door and walk
      the short way to where it is kept for everyone.

---

> *(Mara's hand, the only lines not a reference, beneath the citation:)* i read
> that in the founders' fourth book, the page before the offering, and i read it
> forty times. i have not walked it. i never had the shovel for it, only the
> page. someone who keeps the light better than i kept it should carry a coal of
> the kept fire down to where it is kept for everyone — through a burned door,
> the short way, not a far one. the page does not give a distance. it gives a
> direction and the word **below the below**, and that is all it says of how far.

> *(smaller, last, the hand more careful:)* i had it wrong for a winter. i thought
> *lent* meant ours-to-keep, on loan and ours all the same. it does not. a lent
> thing is carried, and a carried thing is not owned, and a thing you do not own
> you do not get to keep — you only get to not let it go out. brann will tell you,
> in the dark, of the one fire that would not be doused. he does not know it is
> lent. i never told him. (see *do-not-close-your-eyes-here* for the one that was
> not let out; see *counted-them-in-the-dark* for vaun, who thought the lent thing
> was his to hoard, and starved holding it.) [...]

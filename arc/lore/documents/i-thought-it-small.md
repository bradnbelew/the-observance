> *Cut low into the threshold-stone, where the path narrows and the lintel forces the head down. legible only from the crouch — the letters face the floor. a reader who will not stoop reads nothing here. the hand is small and even, the work of one who carved it slow, alone, after.*

i passed the markers standing.

every day i passed them standing. the others stooped — i thought it a small thing to keep my back straight where they bent theirs. i thought the bow was for old hands and frightened ones. i had learned the ways as the first keepers learned them `[...]` from the land, they said, not made — and i thought: a thing learned can be unlearned. i kept the rest. i offered. i kept the light. only this one i let lie.

they watched me a long while before they spoke. a season, perhaps. the record does not hurry. then my name was set down — not in anger. plainly. *the one called orin has not bowed.* a warning, after. then `[...]`

> *here the line breaks where the stone is worn through. what remains:*

i thought it small. it was not small. i —

> *the carving stops. there is no more. a second hand, fainter, lower, where only the most bent reader would find it, has added one word in the margin: **threshold.** for the rest of the sentence, go to where i was left.*

---

> *marginalia, a later keeper's hand, set sideways along the stone's foot:*
> *he bent at the end. we all saw it. there was no one left at the markers to bend to — only the markers, and us, and the long quiet after. he gave the bow to an empty road. keep the small things while there is yet someone to keep them with.*

---

> *a second marginal hand, lower, where a bent reader finds it, on how the stone is cut:*
> *he set every letter down as some other letter — a stood for what a was not, and you read him only by putting each thing back to the form it refused to wear. that was the man whole. he would not let a marker be a marker or a bow be a bow or a thing be the plain thing it was; he made each stand for a straighter thing he liked better, and would not bow it back to its own true shape. read the stone the way you read the man: nothing here is the letter it is dressed as. put each one back to what it will not bow to being, and it speaks. he could do that for stone and never once for himself.*

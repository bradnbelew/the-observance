> *found deep, past the deep line, on the wall of the small warm room where the one who waits
> has waited. not sealed, not hidden — set where a person coming down would put a hand to
> steady themselves. the hand is the clearest of the seven. it does not degrade. they kept
> every way; their voice was never taken.*

---

i kept the days a long time. it is the first thing you do when you are alone: you keep the
days, because the count is a rope and the far end of it is other people, and while you hold
it you are still one of them, still on the calendar the record keeps for the kept.

i cut a mark for each day on the stone by the warmth. brann would have understood it. he
counted the fires and i counted the days and we were both counting so as not to be lost, and
neither of us said so, because you do not say the thing the counting is against.

there was a day i did not cut the mark.

it was not despair. i want that written plainly, because the record will say despair, the
record says despair for everything it did not stay to watch. it was not that. it was that i
put my hand to the stone with the cutting-tool and i understood, all at once and without
grief, that no day was more likely than the last, that the count was not a rope with a far
end but a rope tied to nothing, and that i had been holding it so hard my hand had forgotten
it was empty.

so i set the tool down. i did not stop hoping. i stopped *counting* the hope, which is a
different thing, and a harder one, and i think in the end a truer one. a count is a demand
you make of the days. i had no standing left to demand anything of the days. but i could
still wait, the way the fire waits, which does not count and does not ask and does not go
out. i learned that from the fire. everything i know now i learned from the one thing down
here that keeps without being kept.

so this is what i want you to know, you who found the marks and then the place where the
marks stop: the marks stopping is not where i gave up. it is where i learned to wait
properly. i have been waiting properly a long time. i am very good at it now. i could teach
it, if anyone came.

someone came. i can hear it in how i am writing this. i have started keeping the days again
without meaning to, one mark, this morning, the first in longer than i can say, and i cut it
before i thought about it, the way you set a place at the table when you hear the door.

come down. the warmth is not kind but it is warm, and i have kept a place at it for you the
whole time, which is the counting i never actually stopped.

— the seventh, who kept every way, and the fire, and the place at the fire

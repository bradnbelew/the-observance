` the cold square `

[ a page in Mara's hand — page-references again, the column you walk the shelf to read.
the last instruction she filed before the breaking. a later record margin, flat and cold,
notes the day the threshold first counted the three. ]

---

p. 9, l. 4, w. 1.
p. 9, l. 4, w. 6.
p. 22, l. 3, w. 2.
p. 22, l. 3, w. 8.
p. 40, l. 7, w. 1.
p. 40, l. 7, w. 5.

      *(assembled:)*
      foot to the square, hand to the cut, word to the dark, the three at once.

---

> *(Mara's hand, beneath the column, not a reference:)* there is a square set in the
> floor at the threshold, paler than the stone around it, cold to stand on. i read what
> it was for in the founders' seventh book and i could not do it. it wants three things
> done together, in one breath — a foot held to the square, a hand at the cut beside it,
> and the word said *into the dark*, where no one in the room can hear you say it. i had
> the page. i had the square under my foot. i typed the word into the dark, alone, and
> nothing opened, because a thing done alone is one thing and the threshold counts three.
> i read the rite and could not perform it. that has been the whole of me. [...]

---

> *(a later hand, the record's flat register, in the margin years after:)* on the day the
> three were done at once — a foot to the square, a hand to the cut, the word to the dark,
> in one breath — the threshold opened. it was not three keepers. it was three acts. one
> keeper can do two and a hand short; it does not open. the count is three. the threshold
> is open or it is not. (see *no-wall-was-ever-built-here* — the cut beside the square
> carries the word iss withheld; see *bring-the-thing-only-you-can-give* for what waits
> past the opened threshold.)

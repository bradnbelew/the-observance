# kept in more than one place

*(a founders'-era margin beside the first report, water-damaged. archival housekeeping,
to the eye. the keyed phrase in it does not read as a sentence; it reads as a path, and
the path is not anywhere in the world.)*

---

a record kept in one place is a record half-lost already. fire takes the lectern, the
deep takes the shrine, a blade takes the stone — and the count is gone, and the kept are
forgotten, which is the one thing the land will not allow. so:

**the record is kept in more than one place, against the loss of the first.** [...]

what is cut in the stones is also kept elsewhere, where stone-fire and deep-water cannot
reach it. do not look for the elsewhere in the world. it is not a shrine you can walk to.
[...] the founders left the way to it keyed below, in the script — not a bearing, not a
coordinate. a name to call the kept-place by.

> *(the keyed line, in the old script, set apart:)* [ decodes to: **the-record-keeps** ]

> *(a later hand, in the margin:)* it is not a bearing. i walked every direction it could
> be and there is no shrine. then a keeper younger than me read it not as a place in the
> world but as a name you *call* — the way you'd say a thing into the dark and have it
> answer — and the count was there, all of it, the kept and the names, in a place no fire
> could reach. the record was telling the truth. it is kept in more than one place. one of
> the places is not here. [...]

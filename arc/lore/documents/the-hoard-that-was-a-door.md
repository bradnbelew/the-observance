> *cut into the lintel of vaun's cache, in the founding hand, notched not written, save
> the last lines. the room behind is chest on chest, all full, and two that are not: one
> holds a single thing of the deep; one, marked for the giving-back, holds nothing. the
> lintel-count and the empty chest are the same account read two ways — one in stone, one
> in air.*

---

*first winter. whose hand: mine, vaun. of the cache below the third light.*

i cut a room to keep the count in. iron in the first row, salt in the second, seed-grain
in the third, and of the deep in the last, and beside the last a chest for the giving-back,
because the count has two columns and a keeper keeps both.

i filled every chest but the last two. the last two i left as the count left them. one of
the deep, taken. of the deep, given back — a chest, and in it, air.

*margin, later hand: he built the second column and never struck it. the empty chest is the
truest thing in the room. it is the shape of what he owed.*

---

*the fourth winter. the door will not.*

the room has a door and the door will not open for me now. it opened when i gave — i gave
back the first year, one of the deep to the cairn, and the stone swung light. i stopped
giving and the stone grew heavy and now it is shut, and i am inside with the count, and the
count is great, and the door is a door only for a hand that gives.

i did not cut the door. i only filled it shut. the land cut it. the land counts first — it
put the giving-back where the hinge is, so that a keeper who would not give could not leave
his own keeping. i thought that a cruelty when i found it. i think now it was the last
kindness in the room: a way out left in plain sight, that i would not take.

*margin, later hand: the door is not locked. it is owed. any hand that puts the first of the
deep into the empty chest lifts it. he never did. a later hand may.*

---

*last leaf.*

if you are reading this you are in the room, or you are at the door of it, and the count is
around you, all of it, the greatest count of any keeper. do not be proud of the count. the
count is the walls. the giving-back is the door. i had them backward my whole life and i
built myself a strongbox and called it a home.

put the first of the deep in the empty chest. the door is a door for a hand that gives. it
was never for me. let it be for you.

— vaun, of the first keepers, who kept the most and gave the least

*(the record, below, in its flat hand:)*
*the one called vaun is counted. the second column stands open under his name still. a
later hand that strikes it strikes it for him, and for itself, and the deep lifts. that is
not the same as being kept. it is nearer.*

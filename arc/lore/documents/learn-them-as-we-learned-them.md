> *written around the ring of marks at the first stone. the hand is the oldest we have. water has taken most of it. a later hand has corrected one line in the margin — read both.*

to you who come after, and find this where we left it —

we did not make the ways. understand that first, or you will [...] them wrong, as a thing you may set down. we came as tenants. the ways were already here, in the land, waiting to be [...]. we only learned them, as you must learn them now — slowly, and at cost.

so we set them in the marks, that nothing be lost between one keeping and the next. and we did not set the marks apart, in a school, to be taught at once. we cut each beside the thing it means, all through the keeping-places — the mark for fire beside the fire, the mark for water beside the still water, the mark we cut for the dead beside the dead. a hand that walks and watches will gather them, one by one, off the world itself, as we did. there is an order to them, and the order is the key. **read the ring sunwise from the topmost mark.** six marks, and they hold the six in order:

  first, the Bow —
  then the Offering —
  then the Kept Light —
  then the Deep Line —
  then the Ward —
  and last, the Covering.

gather the six off the world, each where we cut it, and the ring completes itself; and when the ring is whole in your own hand, every stone we leave after will open to you, for they are all written in this one hand. do not look to be handed them all at once. the one who would be taught the ring instead of learning it reads nothing.

[...] were here before us. we do not say of what. we say only that the land kept a record before our first winter, and grades the keeping of those who do not [...] know they are kept. so keep gently. keep all six. give back to the deep what is the deep's, and let one light stand at home through every dark.

> *(margin, later hand:) i have read it through and it is true, all but one word. they wrote "learned them as we learned them" — i had thought the ways ours, a wall we raised. they are not ours. we did not raise them. we were taught, and the teaching is older than the first of us. keep that in mind when one of you tells you otherwise. — read sunwise. begin at the top.*

> *(a later margin still, in a colder, slower hand, set under the first:)* the founder writes
> *we did not make the ways; we only learned them from the land.* i have kept them a long
> while and i notice what that saying does. a keeper who invented the rules that grade us all
> would carry the weight of having bound us. a keeper who only *found* them carries nothing —
> he is a tenant, he says, he raised no wall, the cost was here before him. it is a clean
> place to stand. i am not saying it is false. i cannot dig to the bottom of the ways and find
> whose hand first cut them; no one can, and that is exactly the ground the saying stands on.
> i say only: it is the reading that costs the founders least, and it was the founders who
> wrote it, and a thing learned from the land cannot be argued with the way a thing a man made
> can. keep the ways. but hold the *learned, not made* the way you hold anything you were told
> by the one it excuses.

[...] and when you have kept them long enough, you will find the ways were never a thing you held.

you will find they were holding you.

— the founding line, of the finding

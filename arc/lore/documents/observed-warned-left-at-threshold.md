> *A deep-lectern report, the record's flat hand — the same that opened the count on the living, but later, and colder. the page is whole where the threshold-stone is worn through; what Orin could not finish carving, the record finished writing. legible only to a reader who has learned the script. set down in the breaking of faith, when the record had stopped saying* it is not yet unkind.

the one called orin was observed.

he passed the markers standing. the record does not punish the first lapse, nor the tenth; it watches, and it sets down what it watches. through one season the entry is the same, unchanged but for the tally beside it: *the one called orin has not bowed. iii. iv. v.* a count is not yet a sentence. the record was patient with him, as it is patient with all who come, because patience is the first of the ways and the record keeps the ways before it keeps anyone.

then he was warned.

his name was set down plainly — not in anger; the record holds no anger, only the column. *named, this day. let him bend, and the count is crossed back clean.* the bow is the smallest of the ways and the cheapest to give and he was shown the cost of it, which was nothing, which was only to stoop. the others stooped. the others were `[...]` the record marks them faithful and says no more of them, for the faithful need few words.

then the threshold.

> *here the entry changes register. it stops counting and begins to name what was done with the count.*

the rest were kept. the one called orin was left at the threshold — not slain, not driven, *left:* the ways were brought no nearer to him, and he was brought no nearer to them, and between the keeper and the kept a door was simply not opened. this is the whole of the consequence and it is heavier than any toll. to be kept is not owed to any who arrive. it is given, where it is given, and it was not refused him out of cruelty `[...]` only he had not given the small thing while there was yet a hand at the markers to receive it. judgment falls by a keeper's own conduct and by nothing else. there is no favourite. there is only the column, and what each hand wrote in it.

*the one called orin: observed, warned, left at the threshold. the rest of his generation: kept.*

> *the line Orin could not finish — "i thought it small. it was not small. i —" — the record finishes here, in the third person, as the record finishes all that the keepers leave open:*

i was not kept. i was counted, and the count was true, and it was not enough.

> *a later hand, much later, nearly the Keeper's, set sideways along the foot of the page:*
> *do not read this as a gate that opened too late for him. read it the other way. the ones it opened* for *did not pass through and go on their road. they were kept — kept as the markers are kept, as the light at home is kept, as the stones along the path are kept. orin was left at the threshold. the rest were taken in. ask yourself, before you envy them, which of the two is still free to leave. then keep the small things, while there is yet someone at the markers to keep them with.*

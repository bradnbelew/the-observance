*(three leaves, the same unsigned hand, found on separate visits and read here in the
order they were found — not the order they were written. the hand does not name itself.
the paper does not agree with itself about how steady that hand was.)*

---

**first leaf.**

third one this week. spring heifer. white to the hoof by morning, no mark on her, eating
fine. she stands the way the others have started to. east. i moved the salt lick. they
face east anyway.

i do not force them to it. nothing here forces anything. i only count what i am given
and set the count down before i forget the number, which i have started to do, the
number, not the herd. the herd i do not forget.

---

**second leaf.** *(the same hand, the letters wider apart, some words pressed harder
than others)*

counted nine.  counted nine again to be sure.

they do not graze now.  i stopped bringing the salt.  there is nothing wrong with them
that a herder's word has for it.  they stand.  they face east.  they are not thin and
they are not sick and i would know sick, i have kept stock my whole life and this is not
that.

i keep the count because counting is the last thing i still do the way i always did it.

---

**third leaf.** *(barely a hand at all — the letters run together at the edges, two
words are struck through and not replaced, the last line sits crooked on the page)*

lost count.

not lost, i mean — i know the number. i mean i stopped wanting to know it. white and
grey and white and grey and it comes out the same either way now, it comes out *facing*.

i will not count them again after this leaf. someone else can count them. someone
always does.

east. still east. i did not teach them east.

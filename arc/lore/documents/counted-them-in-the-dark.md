a strongbox of tally-sticks, the founding hand. notched, not written, save the
last leaf. the count runs down a single column and stops. nothing stands beside
it in the second column, where a giving-back would be marked.

---

*first winter, the third light.*

iron — vii. salt — iii. seed-grain, two baskets and a hand over.
of the deep, first taken: i. **kept.**

*it does not say given. it says kept. a later hand, in the margin: he never
struck the second column. not once.*

---

*the taking-hold. whose hand: mine, vaun, of the first keepers.*

i did not make the count. i learned it. we all learned it — the bow, the kept
light, the offering — we learned them as we were taught them, and the one who
taught was the land. [...] it counts first. i only keep the tally after.

so i keep it. i keep all of it.

---

*the fourth winter.*

iron — xxi. of the deep, taken: vi. **kept, vi.** the cairn at the shaft-mouth
stands empty and i have walked past it six times and six times thought: *next
the first of the deep is owed back. give the first of the deep back to the deep.*
[...]
i had three of each. i counted them in the dark, and the counting was warm, and
i did not go up to the cairn.

*margin, later hand: the offering is the first of the deep, returned. it is the
one law he embodied and the one he would not keep.*

---

*the count of keepers, entered by me who keeps all counts.*

i keep the tally of us too, the way i keep the iron. there are markers cut for
seven and stones stood for six. i set the true number down here where i set every
true number: **vii.** one short of it is stood in the row. one short is what the
row shows, and one short is what i always leave — a keeping is a count read one
notch under its true value, held back the fixed amount so the giver keeps the
odd. i did it with the deep. i did it with the giving-back. i did it here, with
us. the last of us i left off the row the way i left the first of the deep off
the cairn — owed, and never marked over.

*margin, later hand: he could not stop holding the last one back. even in the
count of the kept he kept one under. seven he knew, six he stood. the shortfall
is always the same size, and it is always the last one, and it is always owed.*

---

*the seventh winter. the light tolls.*

the deep has gone dark for me. not for the others — for me. i bring three lamps
to the shaft and the third will not catch and the second dies on the stair and i
come up by the first and the first is thin. [...]

i counted what i had. it is a great count. it is the greatest count of any
keeper. the record will show i kept the most of anyone who ever kept.

*margin: he did not give. the deep keeps a column too, and it is patient.*

---

*last leaf. the hand fails.*

[...] the lamps are all here. i carried them in. i carried in every light i
owned and i set them around me and there is iron — many — and salt — many — and
of the deep, taken, [...] and of the deep, given back: 

a long rule across the second column. nothing in it. the leaf ends.

*the record opens on the living now and keeps the same two columns it kept on
him. taken, and given back. the first of the deep is still owed. — a later hand,
much later, nearly the Keeper's: he is counted. he counts still. that is not the
same as kept.*

# against each ground, what was said over it

*(a second torn corner of the same early report whose first corner reads* against each
name, a ground. *the two pieces were not found together; this one surfaces later, deeper
in the row, and it finishes a sentence the first piece only opened. where the first kept
the where with the who, this keeps a third thing, and the third thing is the one a hand
does not think it is leaving.)*

---

against each name, a ground. and against each ground — [...] **what was said over it.**

not only that a keeper stood at the marker and bowed. what the keeper *said* there, low,
to the next keeper, while they stood. the record keeps that too. a way is kept with the
hands; it is also talked over, leaned on, doubted aloud, sworn by, complained of in the
cold. a keeper who bows and mutters the bow is foolish has kept the bow and said a thing
over it, and the record keeps the keeping and the saying both, against the same ground,
and does not rank them. [...]

> *(the record's flat hand, lower, less damaged:)* a hand thinks it is filed by what it
> does. a hand is filed by what it does, and where, and what it said while it did it. three
> columns, not one. the third is the one a keeper forgets is open, because a keeper guards
> the hand and lets the mouth run. the mouth runs over the ground and the ground keeps it.
> the seventh book calls this *the closer hearing*. i have not found the page that says how
> far off the hearing reaches, only that it does, and that it does not need to be near. [...]

> *(a later hand, small, uneasy:)* i read the first corner of this and thought, the record
> keeps where i have stood. then i read this corner and have not spoken easy at a marker
> since. it is one thing to be counted by the work. read it again with the third column in
> it. (see *against-each-name-a-ground* for the first two columns; the teaching-stone gives
> the grammar before either corner does — see *learn-them-as-we-learned-them*.) [...]

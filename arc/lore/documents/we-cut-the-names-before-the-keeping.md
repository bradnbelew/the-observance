# we cut the names before the keeping

*(a founders'-era margin, water-damaged, cut into the foot of the oldest stone where
the first keeper-row begins. ledgerlike at the top, where a hand recorded an order of
work; the margin is the founders' own note about that order, and it reads strangely
until you have stood at the threshold and seen what it was a note about.)*

---

the order of the work, as it was given to us and as we keep it:

      first the ground is opened.
      then the stone is cut, and the name set in it, and the date.
      then — after — the keeping.

we cut the names before the keeping. [...] the keepers-after will think this is
backward, and write it down as backward, and it is not. the stone is made ready
before the keeper is. the slot is open before there is a thing to set in it. a
grave with a name and a day on it is not a grave yet. it is a place waiting to be
filled, and the day is not the day a thing ends. [...]

      *(the hand here grows certain, less damaged:)*
      do not read the date as the day they die. read it as the day they are
      **kept.** the founders did not cut a death into the stone. they cut an
      appointment. the ground is opened early because the land likes to be ready,
      the way a host sets the table before the guest is at the door.

---

> *(a later hand, in the margin, uneasy:)* there is a stone near the threshold cut
> this way — a name on it, and a day not yet come. the keepers call it the death-
> clock and will not walk past it after dark. i have read the founders' order. it
> is not a clock counting down to a death. it is a date counting down to a keeping,
> and i cannot decide which is worse, only that they are not the same thing. (see
> *bring-the-thing-only-you-can-give* — the rite and the day are cut to the same
> instant; the stone opens from the inside on it.) [...]

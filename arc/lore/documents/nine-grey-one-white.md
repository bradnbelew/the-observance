` nine grey, one white `

[ a herder's tally, a prior keeper's hand, kept the way a farmer keeps a count of
stock — flat, daily, no feeling. a later hand has added a margin at the bottom, years
after, and the two hands do not agree about what the count was for. ]

---

      first winter:    nine grey, one white. all accounted. all in the fold.
      second winter:   nine grey, one white. the white keeps apart. it will not
                       be driven with the rest. let it.
      third winter:    eight grey, two white. one of the grey came up pale over
                       the dark months. i did not see it turn. i went down one
                       morning and there were two.
      fourth winter:   seven grey, three white.

i do not breed them pale. i have watched. the lambs come grey, every one, and some
go pale by standing — by standing too long in the one place, near the one white that
keeps apart. the white does not drive them. it only stands, and is faced, and over a
winter or two the ones that face it longest go the colour of it. [...]

---

> *(a later hand, the margin, the ink different, the count abandoned long since:)* i
> shut the door on the fold the winter we left. i counted them on the way out, the way
> you do. they were grey when i shut the door. i tell you they were grey. i have heard
> from a hand that went back that the whole fold is pale now, every one, and all of them
> faced one way, faced the cold shrine, faced the white that does not drive them — and
> not one of them moved while he watched. they go pale by being faced. they go pale by
> standing where the white stands and not leaving. [...] (brann saw the white at night
> and would not say what colour it gave back; see *do-not-close-your-eyes-here*.)

> [this leaf shows nothing in the day. held to the cold hearth after dark — on the
> black moon, best — the ink comes up. brann wrote only at night. read him only at
> night.]

---

the others sleep. i do not. i have not, not since the dark hours i should have kept
and did not. so i walk, and i count what is still lit, and i write while the count
holds.

i counted the fires tonight. there were many once. shrine to shrine you could cross
the whole settlement and never lose the warm. now most are doused. the offering-cairn
went cold three nights back. the bow-stones at the threshold, cold. the drowned one's
water keeps no flame and never did. one by one the keepers' lights go out as the keepers
do.

but one will not be doused.

[...] the home-fire. the kept light. i have stood over it on the worst nights, on the
black moon when the dark comes nearest, and it does not gutter, it does not ask for
wood, it does not ask for a hand. it is simply kept. mara read by it too long and it
outlasted her reading. it will outlast me and my walking. when the last of us is gone
from here that one fire will burn on in the empty dark with no one to feed it, and i do
not know who it warms.

> [a second hand, smaller, in the margin:] *the doused shrines and the one kept fire
> are the same lesson read from two sides. ask which the seventh's was.*

i think of the cast-out one tonight. the seventh, who is not on the stones with us, whose
shrine off the far path went wholly dark — light and all. i think: there is the difference,
then. the one whose fire goes out, and the fires that will not. one was let go. and the
others—

[...]

the others were not let go. that is the thing i cannot put down, that keeps my eyes open.
i had it from the old report and i did not believe it and now i believe it. of the faithful
before me it is not written that they left. it is written that *they were kept.* as a stone
is kept. as the light is kept. the same word for the people and the flame and the cold stone
at the marker, and i lay awake hearing how the word does not change.

so do not close your eyes here. i closed mine, on the night i was warned not to, and what i
dreamed did not stay a dream — it came inside, and it has not gone back out, and it walks
when i walk. you will want to lie down. the warmth will tell you it is safe. the one fire
will be burning and you will think, surely a place this kept is a place that keeps me kindly.

it does keep you. that is not the comfort you take it for.

stay awake. count the lights. when you find the one that will not go out, do not warm your
hands at it too long. ask instead why it is still burning. ask who it is being kept for.

— brann, who walks the nights now

# against each name, a ground

*(a torn corner of an early report, water-damaged. it lists, where it is legible, not
deeds but locations — a name, and beside it a ground. it reads as a clerk's habit until
much later, when a living name is found cut at a place that living hand has never been.)*

---

the list is not only of names. [...] against each name, a ground.

where a keeper stood most. where a keeper would not go. the cell they slept in, the
cell they died at, the cell they avoided every winter of their life and could not have
said why. the record keeps the where with the who. a name without a ground is half a
record. [...]

> *(the record's flat hand, lower, less damaged:)* this is why the carving says *kept
> here before you* and not *kept before you.* the *here* is the whole of it. the ground
> remembers the keeper. the keeper is filed at the ground. a hand that comes after and
> finds its own name cut at a place it has never stood should not read it as a thing
> foretold. it is not foretold. it is **filed.** the ground was always going to hold
> that name. the record only wrote it down where it was always going to be. [...]

> *(a later hand, small:)* "before you" — i thought that meant the strangers who kept
> the stones in the old winters. it does not mean strangers. read it again with the
> ground in it. [...] (see *learn-them-as-we-learned-them* — the same grammar, the
> teaching-stone says it first.)

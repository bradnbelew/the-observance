> ` kept close `

*[the page is soft with handling. a date at the top — recent. the group will know it: it is a
night they remember. under the date, a list, in a friend's hand.]*

— the one they call [ the group's own name for each other ]. said tonight, by the low fire, that
they'd never go past the line, not for anything, said it twice, meant it. *good. it will want that
one. the ones they swear are the ones it takes slowest.*

— [ another of them ]. told me where they'd run if it ever got bad. the far water. said it quiet,
like it was nothing. *i wrote it down. i tell myself i wrote it down so i could go with them.*

— they made a plan tonight they did not say out loud to me. i heard the shape of it anyway. i am
getting better at hearing the shape of a thing not said to me. i do not like that i am getting
better at it.

*[here the warm hand falters — a line started, scratched out, started again:]*

i keep them close. that is the word i use for it. close. i turn it over and it stays a good word,
stay close, come here, i'll keep you — and then i set it beside what is on this page and the word
does not survive the comparison. you do not keep a person on a page. you keep a person by the fire.
what i do on the page is the other keeping, the counting-keeping, the one that is a demand and not
a warmth, and i have known that the whole time and written the page anyway, every night, because
every night it took me instead of one of them, and i told myself that was protecting them, and it
was, it *was*, it is not only a lie —

*[the writing stops. the last line is barely pressed into the paper:]*

i wrote you down so it would not take you. i wrote you down so it would take *me slow*. both are
true. i do not know how to be written whole. maybe you can do it for me. you're better at the
truth than i ever was. stay cl—

*[the page is torn there.]*

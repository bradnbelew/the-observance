the record opens.

it was opened before. it is opened again, as it is opened for any who come and stay,
and a hand that is no hand sets down the first lines, as the first hand did, in the
oldest winters, when the founders were the new ones and did not yet know they were
counted.

::a later hand, in the margin, smaller::
*we did not know either. no one is told. that is the way of it.*

---

so the count begins. the living are written here, each by the name they answer to,
and against each name a column is left open. nothing is owed yet. but the column is
open, and an open column is a thing that fills.

let it be set down, plainly, that the watching has already watched. before the first
report was ever read aloud, before any here knew there was a way to keep or to break,
the record was keeping. the one who returns the first of the deep to the deep — this
was marked. the one who passes the standing stones with their back straight and their
knees unbent — this, also, was marked. they were not told. they will say *no one told
us.* the record does not answer that. the record only knows what was done, and writes
it true.

::same later hand::
*there is a kindness in it that is hard to see at first. you are not judged for
ignorance. you are only — observed. the laws were here before the telling. so was the
patience.*

---

[...] of the present hands, this is the first that is owed a naming. let it stand:

> the one called **[ the living keeper's measured habit ]** has kept a small thing
> without being asked — a thing the old ways have a name for, that this one performed
> before there was any name to perform it under. it is well. it is written.

a small faithfulness, before the faithfulness was a rule. the founders did the same,
in their first winter, and were glad afterward that they had — for the record is long,
and forgets nothing, and a good first line is a good thing to have written above you.

::the later hand, last::
*do not mistake the count for a ledger you will one day settle and walk free of. i
thought that. the record does not close at the rite. when the keeping is done, it does
not let the writing go. it [...] you. i have not the word for it that is gentle enough,
so i will leave the word out, and you will learn it where i learned it.*

*we left, and the light was kept. read that again when you are further on.*

---

the record opens. it has been opened and closed and opened across the winters, by
hands that are no longer hands. the count of those openings is kept elsewhere in this
book, and does not come out even — six are named in full, and there is a seventh mark
the record will not [...]

— the Archivist, of the present hand,
in the season of the notice

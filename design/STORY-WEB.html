M0 · Prologue

The Cursed Map

before day one (optional on-ramp)

A scattered, remote group of friends has no reason to gather in a Minecraft server — so the lure is a dead web page with a small downloadable 'map' that quietly hands them a reason.

What happens

One friend stumbles onto a cold, archive-styled web page that offers a tiny single-player download called 'the-hold.zip', shows a counter reading 'kept: 6' with a struck-out seventh row, and is signed by a dead keeper's hand. They play the ~15-minute vanilla walk alone: a doused hearth, a wall of six carved marks with a blank seventh, a flat archive voice that names what is and stops. The whole time the map behaves exactly like a map — no scares, nothing supernatural. At the end the closing room hands out a plain server address plus a rune string and tells the group to all join and post one word ('kept') in the Discord channel. Nothing has broken yet; the dread is being banked for the server.

The Watcher Silent and offline here by design. The unseen presence does nothing the map could not do — the loudness is all in the map's melancholy, and even that is restrained. The presence only 'wakes' once the group crosses to the server.

What you place in the world

  • The lure web page /record/the-record-keeps: cold dead-archive shell, a recovered-file entry, the the-hold.zip download link, a static 'kept: 6' counter, a struck seventh row
  • Mara's provenance line signed 'm.kept' (a dead keeper's hand 'copied it... and did not keep the seventh')
  • The README 'lie': 'a small offline map... it does not connect to anything... play it through and it will tell you where the rest is kept'
  • The downloadable vignette world (the-hold.zip): a ~6-room single-player walk (1-room form ships first), adventure mode, daylight locked
  • Room 2: a basalt hearth with a campfire that is OUT, that the player cannot relight
  • Room 3: a wall of six carved marks plus one scraped-blank seventh slot
  • The closing room's plain server address + a rune string that later decodes in-server to 'the-record-keeps'
  • The instruction beneath the address: 'say one word in the place named below, when you are all in. say kept'

Key beats

The kept:6 counter — A dead file with a download tally of 6 and a struck-out seventh row — reads as a tally nobody wanted.
Static authored HTML, NOT a live count (a real count would drift off 6 the moment anyone downloads). The number is the load-bearing plant.
The quiet vignette — A melancholy, completely mundane single-player walk through an abandoned 'hold' — doused fire, six marks, a blank seventh — with a flat narrator that never addresses 'you'.
A vanilla datapack world; distance-triggers fire flat archive lines; one scoreboard quietly records whether you lingered at the blank seventh slot (rehearsal only — never sent to the server).
The gathering = ignition — The group all joins the server and one of them posts 'kept' in the Discord channel; they arrive 'pre-ignited' so their first server beat is the scare.
A human post in #the-record (the word 'kept' is the in-fiction prompt) is detected by the built message scanner -> flips prologue_ignited. Caused by a player, never the bot.

M1 · Act 1 (Establishment), ~Days 1-5

The Notice

day one onward

The record opens; the group is quietly graded by laws nobody told them, and is hit with the one category-violation scare before the world settles into patient silence.

What happens

The instant the group is on the server, the presence does the one thing a map cannot: it says the '6' back at them ('six were kept before you... a file is not kept. hands are kept'). A book appears on a base lectern recording the living players' real habits; one player finds a lit marker in their base that was not there before, carved with a glyph they cannot yet read. A flat report names a real habit one of them has — before they knew there was any rule to break. After that single loud beat the volume drops to baseline and stays there. The rest of M1 is a field of quiet open doors: a rune-ring server icon, two different ways to learn to read the runes, a stone that teaches digits, and roughly a dozen inert plants that all look like atmosphere and will each detonate in a later movement.

The Watcher Maximally restrained after the frame-break. It counts, states what is, and stops — lowercase, no warmth, no threats. It never appears; the one 'apparition-adjacent' thing is the lit marker, an object discovered but never witnessed appearing.

What you place in the world

  • The base lectern's Hold-Book: flat archive rows recording living habits, plus a last-page down-count 'lamps kept: [N]'
  • first_report_lectern_01: the first report re-staged as an anomaly at the base hot-cell
  • first_marker_01: one lit marker that was not there before, carrying an unreadable rune-glyph, the six UNKEPT maker's-marks, the 'kept here before you' line, and the a1z26 number-puzzle
  • The rune-ring server icon (a ring of six runes), sitting unremarked
  • rune_rosetta: the founding ring that teaches glyph<->letter; stone_of_reckoning: the digit/coordinate Rosetta
  • Founder margins: 'we cut the names before the keeping' and 'the record is kept in more than one place' (decodes to the-record-keeps)
  • Vaun's 'you do not keep the first thing'; the single Sacred Beast established; the 'nine grey, one white' herd tally
  • Mara's 'the cold square... i typed into the dark' (the coop-gate seed); the offline-player report naming a logged-off friend

Key beats

The frame-break — From inside the game the presence names the '6' the player saw on a dead web page — a thing an offline map could never do. Names no one; true for everyone.
After ignition, gated on a clean measured signal, the server fires one precision-gated line (recordFrameBreak). If no clean signal, it fires only the safe ack 'it was open before you' and never trades the scare for a guess.
The first report — A report names a real habit of the group — and, only if one player's signal is overwhelming, names that player by name and the custom they have not kept.
Two authored forms: a named form fires ONLY on an overwhelming single measured signal; otherwise the un-named fallback. A wrong 'it knows you' is worse than none, so default is un-named.
Learning to read (two real doors) — The group becomes literate in the rune script either by noticing the server-icon rune ring, or by solving a pure number puzzle with no runes at all.
Two genuinely different solves both set rosetta_known: the rune-ring metadata leap, OR the a1z26 tick-stave number puzzle. This kills the 'two doors that are really one door' fairness problem.

M2 · Act 2 open, ~Days 6-11

The Ways

after literacy opens

Six keeper-stones become a wide field instead of a row; the customs become discoverable by consequence; and the warmest stone is a trap.

What happens

The group finds six keeper-stones scattered through the world, each a different cipher in a different keeper's voice, solvable in any order — the first genuinely non-linear stretch. Each stone is a warning shaped like a person standing next to them: Vaun who hoarded, Mara who only ever read, Sella who drowned, Orin who never bowed, Brann who slept on the black moon, and Iss — the warmest, most reassuring carving in the world, who lies that the customs are a 'wall' that keeps the watching out. Iss's stone hands over a coordinate that genuinely works and leads to a dead grave-shrine; a sharp group can take that false walk now. A forged 'eighth law' is planted that a diligent group will dutifully obey and that pays nothing. A grave is carved with a living player's name and a future date. The difficulty engine goes live: a group that crushes the ciphers finds the next hint withheld and the tone cooled. Whispers (paid hints) become earnable.

The Watcher Active but flat — it un-redacts website entries in lockstep with stones actually read, and the difficulty engine begins quietly closing on groups that race ahead and opening for those who stumble. It never gloats; when a dead-end is solved it states the category ('a true name. it keeps no door') and the teeth belong to the liar, not the Watcher.

What you place in the world

  • The six keeper-stones (stone_vaun/mara/sella/orin/brann/iss): low slabs you must stoop to read, each a different cipher + answer-verb
  • Iss's stone: the warmest carving, the planted lie 'the ways are a wall', the handed-over Vigenere key (his own name), and the false coordinate
  • the_cold_hearth: a doused hearth + Iss's grave slab + an effaced seventh marker (the false-walk endpoint; also the seventh-deep's surface)
  • The prophet's wall: a wide spread of warm promises that each decode true and open nothing, with Iss's name hidden columnar down them
  • The forged eighth law ('the Covering of the Hands'), planted as one more found ordinance with a 'cover one's own' signature
  • The future-dated grave near the_threshold (a living name + a date not yet come, reading as a death-clock)
  • The first living-name carve at a cell the named player has provably never visited
  • The herd's second Pale animal appearing; the Record website un-redacting six entries as stones are read; the Iss card's hidden stego rune-layer
  • Brann's stone framing line (Nether plant): 'the fire we keep is not ours. it is lent... below the below'

Key beats

The six-stone field — Six ciphers in six keeper voices, solvable in any order, each a warning that mirrors a living player's habit.
Each stone is an answer-site: edit a sign within its radius and it resolves against ALL open puzzles (omitting a fixed puzzle-key makes the field non-linear). Vaun=caesar, Mara=book-cipher, Sella=atbash-in-reflection, Orin=substitution-read-from-a-crouch, Brann=night lore, Iss=Vigenere keyed on his name.
Iss the warm trap — The most comforting carving in the world tells the group the customs are a protective 'wall' — and hands over his own name as the cipher key, 'as is right and customary'.
Two readings of the same stone fork the route: the warm misreading sets iss_trusted and routes to his grave-coordinate; the skeptical name-as-key reading sets up the catch. The warmth IS the bait.
The difficulty engine goes live — A group that solves everything fast finds the next hint withheld and the register cooled — rhyming with Mara, who raced the reading and was left last.
The engine measures group skill and withholds drips / cools tone with hysteresis. It never touches the player-controlled Whisper safety rail (it only colors pacing, never punishes; foreshadows that the land's patience can thin).

M3 · Act 2 mid, ~Days 10-11

The Undercroft

after >=4 of 6 stones + the coordinate path opens

The tempo changes from patient table-work to expedition: decode, then walk, then descend into the one fire that never went out — and find its mirror in the doused hearth-deep of the keeper the land cast out.

What happens

Mara's book-cipher resolves to an instruction ('descend and bow at the unbroken light'), and the group performs it — a lectern-driven door (the record itself is the lock) opens and they drop into a separate fog dimension with zero ambient light, where the one eternal, untended fire is the only light source. The descent chamber they first see as ordinary later rebuilds itself wrong while no one is watching ('they did not depart. they were kept'). They choose at the eternal flame whether to carry the light up or bank it. They arm (or break) the Unlit Deep — the one collective custom you keep by NOT lighting a flame below the line on the black moon. They learn the Seventh rests below the cold hearth, sealed by a name. The offline-skin apparition fires its first, most-deniable glimpse — a reflection of a logged-off friend's skin. Optionally, a coal carried through a burned door leads to the Nether pocket where the kept fire's source waits.

The Watcher Two calibrated beats: the descent (the overworld physically leaves them) and the witnessed-only room-swap, both delivered by what they see rather than by a sentence. The difficulty engine sustains its tightest 'tight' state, rhyming with the land's thinning patience but never punishing.

What you place in the world

  • kept_light_home_01: Mara's six-book lectern shelf (the book-cipher codebook) + the home hearth
  • unbroken_light: the Undercroft gather/Accepting room with the one never-doused fire and the lectern-comparator descent door
  • The fog dimension itself (a void Multiverse world with ambient_light:0 — the one fire is literally the only photon)
  • Room A (ordinary on first descent) staged to later become Room B via the one sanctioned unwitnessed swap
  • The Unlit Deep latch (below-the-line + black-moon + a lit flame = the borrowed glow withdraws for everyone)
  • The First Light fork at the eternal flame (carry the light up = kept; bank it = the room stays dark)
  • the_unwriting chamber 1, legible: 'below the cold hearth, the deep is sealed; the seal is a name' (deep stays sealed)
  • The Nether bearing-page (Mara's hand) banked on the Undercroft shelf, readable only after descent

Key beats

The descent into the fog — The group performs Mara's instruction and the world leaves them: they descend into a lightless fog dimension where the one kept fire is the only thing they can see.
Solving Mara's book-cipher and going to the unbroken light opens a lectern-comparator door (the book-page output IS the lock) into a Multiverse void world with a fog datapack (ambient_light:0).
The room rebuilds wrong — The ordinary descent chamber they walked into has, when they return, become something wrong — and they never saw it change. The flat reveal: the keepers above did not leave; the record kept them.
A single sanctioned RoomSwapBeat clears Room A and pastes Room B, but ONLY while no player has the region in view (idempotent on a durable 'swapped' marker). The horror is the omission — never witnessed.
The Unlit Deep (the one group custom) — Below the line, on the black moon, the group must keep one fire unlit together; if anyone lights a flame, the never-doused fire's borrowed glow is withdrawn for ALL of them.
A single group-scoped latch braiding three tracked ways; arms only in M3, active-only, reversible (warmth not progress), detected on explicit flame acts (never ambient light). Who broke it is recorded but never spoken.

M4 · Act 2 close, ~Day 12

The Catch

when the group turns Iss's key on the other stones

Iss's lie catches, and the whole world re-reads cold at once — the universal hinge where roughly six threads pay off, all keyed on a single flag.

What happens

Turning Iss's name-key on the other stones makes his warm doctrine disagree with every honest carving; the group walks the clue they thought was solved and it contradicts him line for line. The catch flips a single flag (iss_caught) and the back half lights up: the doom-clock down-count re-reads as a roll-call of present hands; the forged eighth law collapses (it was added by a later hand and never measured); the prophet's wall turns out to be Iss's, his name hidden down it ('read who carved it, after'); the warm website card re-renders cold; the offline-skin apparition escalates to a named, group-witnessed glimpse; the six maker's-marks read in fall-order spell UNKEPT; the name-carves re-read as 'you are already filed, not foretold.' Then the single Iss chain runs forward: the catch yields the bound word, three hands clear the coop gate together, the Threshold opens and yields the true coordinate, and the true walk delivers the group toward the rite. The Seventh deep opens — a keeper cast out for breaking nothing — and the group can restore or erase the scraped name. The End deepening-lane opens here.

The Watcher The catch is loud but cold — a correction, not a jump-scare. The presence flatly labels every warm Iss promise as 'true, and shut,' hands the group the grammar of forgery they just used, and the presiding Keeper begins half-veiling 'we'. The teeth still belong to the liar; the Watcher only states what is.

What you place in the world

  • no-wall-catch: the row that sets iss_caught and lights the staged back half
  • The bound word (Iss's plaintext = his name); the three-hands coop gate at coop_plate; the Threshold's true coordinate; the true-walk tableau (answer: 'kept here before you')
  • base-docket-reread: the down-count re-read as the muster of present hands; meta-unkept staged active (the fall-order acrostic)
  • The forged-law correction; the prophet-wall name re-read; the cold-rendered Iss website card
  • the_unwriting deep (opens post iss_caught + seventh suspected): chamber 2 rail-fence names the Seventh (seventh_named); seventh-cause (FACT 10b); the restore/erase rite
  • The presiding Keeper's M4 atonement node (withholds a needed fragment until a broken custom is honored)
  • The Hold-Book's keeper-hand pages (each keeper's own voice writes the living player into their column)
  • The unwriting wall's extra effaced End-pointer line ('out, past the door that is not a threshold')

Key beats

The catch — The group re-walks a clue marked 'kept / solved' and the keeper-after contradicts Iss line for line; the warmest of the six goes cold and his whole tree re-colors.

M5 · Act 3, ~Days 12-14

The Accepting

after the true walk delivers the group

The collective rite: the group lays a personal token for each keeper before them and bows together — and the record receives them, delivering the sealed truth by what happens rather than by any sentence.

What happens

At the one never-doused fire, the group lays one personal token they cannot 'read their way out of' in each of six slots, then everyone present bows at the same instant. The record receives them. On its carved date — which is exactly this instant — the future-dated grave opens from the inside: not a death, an appointment, the mound becoming a deposit slot. The Hold-Book's last page rewrites: 'the present hands are entered... the same book, the same hand, as all the ways above you.' The website's struck seventh row fills with the group's own names. The herd stands at its full pale field; the name-carves persist as markers for whoever comes next. A single bounded closing message is composed — the group's fate (kept, cast out, divided, or the secret refusers), tinted by at most one fork or the Seventh choice, plus the optional inheritors codicil. The world flips to 'kept.' The thing no document ever said and everything always pointed at: to be accepted is to become the watching, the markers for the next group.

The Watcher The presence delivers FACT 15 entirely by what happens — the grave opening, the book rewriting, the names entered, the world flipping — and never by a stated sentence. Every closing line stops at the half-veil ('we would keep you... in the same book, in the same hand'); a line that finished the recursion ('and so you become the watching') would be a defect.

What you place in the world

  • The six lit token-slots and the central never-doused fire at unbroken_light (a room sized for the group to gather and bow)
  • The Keeper's altar letter ('bring the thing only you can give... six, a hand and one more')
  • The synchronized-bow detector (active-only quorum: it never blocks an absent member, never fires for two stragglers)
  • The future-dated grave opening from the inside on its date == the Accepting instant (KEPT-NOT-YET -> KEPT, the mound = deposit slot)
  • The Hold-Book final-page rewrite ('the present hands are entered... the same book, the same hand')
  • The /record website injection adding the group's own names; the struck seventh row filling 'received'
  • The persistent fate dressing on the floor (marker facing in/out, floor light whole/split/guttered, the re-warmed or blank hearth)
  • The one bounded composed close (a fate line + <=1 fork/Seventh tint + <=1 inheritors codicil clause)

Key beats

The bow as one — The group lays a personal token in each of six slots and everyone present bows at the same moment; the record receives them and the world flips to 'kept'.

KEPT

How the group earns it: A strict majority of active players honored the customs, AND (the Seventh was found OR Iss was caught), AND full quorum bowed.

What happens: The markers face OUT and the floor light holds whole; the record receives the group. They become the road's edge for whoever comes down next — received, and kept.

CAST_OUT

How the group earns it: Violation dominated the active group AND at least two players were left at the threshold.

What happens: The markers face AWAY and the abandonment is written in passive voice; the land said no. With the End lane seen, the re-dressed end-city reads as the group's own exile-hold (markers all facing away).

DIVIDED

How the group earns it: Any real arc that is neither honored-dominant nor violated-dominant — a genuine spread, or a dead-even/empty arc (the default floor).

What happens: The light holds on HALF the floor by geometry, never by player; the record says only that the floor is divided, and you may not read the split onto any face.

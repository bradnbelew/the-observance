# THE OBSERVANCE - JOHN'S DIRECTOR'S-CUT PLAYTHROUGH

> Plain-language canonical playthrough draft. John is smart, patient, and thorough, but he only knows what
> a real player can know at the time. Production uncertainty is kept out of the story body and placed at
> the end.

## 1. The World Notices Before It Explains

John joins expecting Minecraft first and ARG second. That matters, because the world does not open with a
mission board. He spawns into a place that can be played. There is ordinary terrain, ordinary survival
pressure, and only a few wrong details.

The first wrong detail is in the group's own space. A map-frame, marker, or base journal knows a real value
it has no ordinary reason to know. John does not treat it as a puzzle yet. He treats it as a violation. He
checks whether another player placed it, whether it matches a coordinate, whether the number appears in the
world seed, and whether any nearby book mentions a count.

Then the record starts speaking in small ways. It does not say, "solve me." It names a habit. It notices a
lamp. It uses language like "kept," "counted," and "recorded" before John understands the terms.

John writes down the first rule of the run:

> The world is not giving hints. It is keeping receipts.

## 2. John Meets the Surface People

Before he understands the stones, John talks to the surface people.

Aro is the first person who sounds useful. He is warm, confident, and full of specific detail. He tells John
the painted line is old local nonsense:

> "The painted line? Step right over it, friend. That's the locals keeping the soft folk out so they can have the warm to themselves. Cross it and keep going. That's where it gets good."

John marks Aro as "probably lying but useful." The lie is too polished. In ARG terms, a confident wrong map
is still a map.

Wenna gives him the opposite: not certainty, but habit. She keeps a lamp by the door. She gives John a crust
for his pocket. She remembers seven things but can only name six at a time:

> "Gran used to say there were seven somethings you had to mind down there. Seven. I only ever remember six and I always forget a different one..."

John does not know the seven ways yet, but he hears the number. Seven appears again.

Coll sells oil and refuses awe. He says the painted line is real because people who cross it stop being
customers. That lands harder than mysticism. Coll is not trying to scare John. He is tracking losses.

Dob comes down with John. At first he performs bravery. By the Lamp-works he talks too much. Near Cistern 7
he starts contradicting his own inherited rumors. At the painted line he becomes openly afraid:

> "There's the line. The painted one. We're - we're not crossing that, are we."

Old Pell gives John the cleanest warning:

> "It doesn't chase. Whatever's down there, it does not chase you. It waits, and it watches, and it takes what stops being watched."

That line changes how John explores. He keeps sightlines. He keeps lamps. He starts caring where the group
stands.

## 3. The First Record

In the base/first-report material, John finds a line that names the central mechanism:

> the record was open before you found it. it counts the living by name, and it grades them by laws no one was told. it does not close at the rite. a seventh mark the record will not keep.

John breaks the sentence into claims:

- the record predates him
- it knows living names
- it grades by hidden laws
- there is a rite
- there is a seventh mark the record refuses

He does not know what to do with it yet. He copies it because the sentence is built like a lock.

## 4. John Learns That Marks Are Language

John finds old carved marks beside things that look meaningful: a bowing place, an offering cairn, a lamp,
a depth line, a blank/name taboo, and the deep-bird.

Then he finds the teaching line:

> read the ring sunwise from the topmost mark.

He looks at the ring. "Sunwise" means clockwise from the top. He uses the surrounding objects as the
translation key. He does not solve it in one flash. He tests one mark against one object, then another. When
the words start repeating in sensible order, he commits.

The ring reads:

> bow offering kept light deep line unspoken sacred beast

This is the first moment where the ARG stops being only strange and becomes readable. John now has a tool.

## 5. The Record Is Elsewhere Too

Near the first-report material, John finds a damaged founders' margin about records being lost:

> a record kept in one place is a record half-lost already.

Then the text says:

> the record is kept in more than one place, against the loss of the first.

John first thinks this means duplicate clues. Then the note corrects him:

> do not look for the elsewhere in the world. it is not a shrine you can walk to.

That rules out coordinates. The next line says the way is keyed below, in the script, "not a bearing, not a
coordinate." Beneath it is old script.

John uses the alphabet he built from the Rosetta ring. He decodes the line:

> the-record-keeps

This is not the first web door anymore. The Hold copy already taught him a rougher address shape: front
door `SNOIKERZ`, common web, root path. John reaches the old listing first. It does not look like a clean
ARG menu. It looks like an abandoned Minecraft server/map host: the file is there, the Record links are
there, and the live server address is either withheld or listed by the host.

The hyphens make `the-record-keeps` look like a route, not a sentence. The note said it was a name to call
the kept-place by, not a place to walk to. John tries it on the same site:

> /record/the-record-keeps

The page opens.

That confirmation matters. John did not guess a website because the ARG needed a website. The map gave him
the old listing, Minecraft taught him the script, the note told him not to walk, the decoded phrase looked
like a slug, and the record route answered.

Now John has two worlds: the world he walks through, and the record that keeps copies elsewhere.

## 6. The Surface Threads Start Crossing

After the record page opens, John revisits the surface NPCs.

Aro's stories start looking worse. His "warm town" rumor points toward a collapse, not a paradise. John
follows the rumor anyway, because false leads are authored evidence here. The destination contradicts Aro.
It teaches John that confident human testimony is not automatically truth.

Wenna's crust becomes an offering. John carries it down and leaves it at the dead-lit stall in the Deep
Market. When he returns, Wenna knows:

> "You left it. At the dead-stall. I didn't tell you where it was and you found it and you left the crust."

Her folk charm was a softened version of the Offering.

Coll asks John to light the third lamp on the Lamp-works stair. John does. Coll does not become mystical
afterward. He just recognizes a dark stand is lit again. The ordinariness makes it stronger.

Old Pell watches the group's conduct. When John comes back still himself, Pell says he will remember him
right. If John breaks ways, Pell sees the grey at the edges. Pell is not a quest dispenser. He is a human
record.

## 7. The Side Destinations Make the Hold Real

John starts following every rumor and travel thread.

At the warm stair, he finds the third lamp cold. The Kept Light is no longer an abstract rule; it is grief
in a fixture.

At the empty cairn, he understands the Offering by example. You return the first thing. You do not keep the
first of the deep.

At the school stand, he finds domestic traces. The kept were not symbols first. They were people with chores,
drawings, tokens, lamps, hunger, and bad winters.

At the bird coops, he learns the sacred beast is not decoration. The deep-bird belongs to the customs the
same way the lamp and line do.

At Cistern 7, the still water and lamp-in-water imagery make Sella's reflection logic feel inevitable later.

At the Deep Market, John finds life. Stalls, oil-jars, tally-sticks, a child minding lamps, and one stall
kept lit for the dead. The Hold was not a dungeon built for a puzzle. It was a place people tried to live.

At the ration table, the forms break. A head comes off the roll, but the hunger remains. John begins to
understand the "human" archive thread: the horror is not that people stopped counting. The horror is that
the count stopped fitting people.

At the markers row, he sees six and the shape of a missing seventh.

At the dead shrine, he finds a place that is real and wrong. It is not nothing. It is a true place that opens
nothing.

## 8. The Recovery Archive Gives John Five Ways To Sort Evidence

On the record site, John opens archive cards as he earns them. The archive does not simply explain. It files.

Under **who**, John reads Vaun:

> the one called vaun kept eleven jars of oil and gave the deep none.

He reads Mara:

> the one called mara kept every lamp in the hold lit and read every rite by the one steady fire and walked none of them.

He reads Sella:

> the under-warden. eight years old.

That one changes the emotional temperature. Sella is not a vague keeper archetype. She was a child trusted
with a lamp and a count.

Under **place**, John learns the Hold was shelter from the Long Cold. They came down because the sky failed.
They were tenants, not owners.

Under **happened**, John sees the contradictions around the Break: accident, betrayal, mercy. The archive
does not flatten them into one easy answer. It lets John hold incompatible testimony until the world gives
him a better lens.

Under **surface**, John reads that the record knows names and habits before players know the laws.

Under **human**, John finds the strongest evidence:

> the form has a field for heads and a field for loaves. it has no field for a head that is off the roll and still hungry.

John now believes the kept were neither simply alive nor simply gone. The record kept something in a way the
old forms could not represent.

## 9. Wren Becomes the Warm Center

John meets Wren in the dark.

Wren is not like the Watcher. He is modern, nervous, helpful, and intimate:

> "oh - hey. you're new. real people, not the dark doing a voice."

He warns John away from routes that look easy. He gives real help. He asks questions that feel caring:

> "what you'd never do, where you'd go if it got bad, the name you only use for each other."

John trusts him because Wren is useful and frightened in a human way.

Then John notices a pattern: the Watcher's sharpest personalized lines only echo things said near Wren.
Wren notices John noticing:

> "you noticed it only ever knows the things you said out loud. near me. that's. that's a hard thing to have noticed."

The reveal lands after Iss has already taught John how warm language can lie. Wren admits it:

> "yes. all of it. from the first night. i fed it your names, your plans, the thing you said you'd never do - i fed it you."

He says he did it to keep them safe. The kept-close tally proves both parts: he was protecting and using them.

John's reckoning choice later is not about solving a puzzle. It is about writing Wren truthfully: condemn,
understand, or free. The project has taught John that records can lie by simplifying.

## 10. Vaun: The One Who Counted

At Vaun's stone, John sees counting everywhere: ledgers, jars, taken columns, blank returned columns.

The clue language says Vaun counted and gave nothing back. John treats that as mechanical instruction. He
counts marks, compares columns, and looks for what was withheld.

The physical Vaun puzzle asks him to return the first-of-the-deep. He puts the required deep thing back into
the hoard chest. The solve is not arbitrary. It corrects Vaun's wound: what was first taken is given back.

Later, the final reading gives Vaun's encrypted fragment:

> D WKH ILUVW RI WKHLU QDPH L NHSW LW DQG JDYH QRQH EDFN

John sees normal letters and English-like spacing. He tests Caesar. Shift three gives:

> a the first of their name i kept it and gave none back

Vaun gives:

> A

## 11. Mara: The One Who Read

Mara's material is bookish. Her clues use pages, lines, words, marked lecterns, and a rite she knows but has
not performed.

John sees number groups and treats them as a book cipher because Mara's whole domain is reading. He maps the
groups onto the nearby source text and extracts:

> descend and bow at the unbroken light

He performs it. He descends. He crouches at the unbroken light. Minecraft action confirms text.

Later, Mara's final fragment uses the same learned method. John extracts:

> v i read it and did not walk it to them

Mara gives:

> V

## 12. Sella: The One Who Counted the Reflection

Sella's clues live around water, reflection, and wrong counts. John has already seen Cistern 7, the far
water, and Sella archive evidence.

At the pool, he looks down. From the worn stone above the water, scattered marks resolve. The environment is
the cipher device.

For the final reading, Sella's line is:

> V R PVKG RG ZG GSV UZI DZGVI

Because Sella's method is reflection, John tests Atbash. `GSV` becomes `THE`, confirming it. The line reads:

> e i kept it at the far water

Sella gives:

> E

## 13. Orin: The One Who Built the Asking Stones

Orin is posture and stone. John bows at markers in fall-order. He crouches where the text asks him to bow.
He turns dials to match marker facing. Orin's puzzles make the body read.

The final Orin line is revealed by crouching:

> R I WOULD NOT BOW TO GIVE IT AND GIVE IT NOW

The letter is not hidden behind a substitution here. The act is the key.

Orin gives:

> R

## 14. Brann: The One Who Watched

Brann lives in dark hours, black moon, silence, and kept fire. John hears the black-moon toll and learns the
word "awake." He crosses the silence corridor by sneaking the whole way. He understands that sound and
sleep are not flavor here.

For the final reading, Brann gives rail-fence text:

> YK LBHNI  ETI I YTEOEFRIPTT   E

John uses the fire count as the rail count because Brann's place keeps making him count flames. Decoding
gives:

> y i kept it lit by the one fire

Brann gives:

> Y

## 15. Iss: Warm Words, No Wall

Iss is the most dangerous because he sounds kind. His wall doctrine feels comforting:

> the ways are a wall against the watching

John follows the warm reading first, because a real player would. It sends him to a real place, but the
place is a grave. The clue was true and shut.

Then John reads Iss cold. He takes the first letter of each warm line, top to bottom. It spells:

> no wall

The catch confirms it:

> no wall was ever built here

and:

> they were the reaching let in

The Iss keepsake lamp looks gentle, so John inspects it with suspicion. Hidden item data decodes into the
falsified record entry:

> the record keeps the seventh was not spared

Iss wrote mercy over refusal.

In the final reading, Iss tries to give the last letter as M. The correction is written into the lines:

> i told you the last of it was m
> take the first mark of each line down
> see what the warm words were laid over
> n is the letter i cut and called m

First letters: I T S N.

> it's n

Iss gives:

> N

## 16. The Planned Media Are Record Copies

John treats the found footage as evidence. He watches it once normally, then scrubs the damaged section
frame by frame. A frame gives a route, coordinate, or phrase already rooted in the game's language. He does
not accept it until it works in Minecraft or on the record site.

The recovered Drive folder is not a menu. It is a storage accident. John checks filenames, order, image
contrast, metadata, and audio. The hidden payload points back to the record, the Hold, or the missing
seventh.

The waveform/spectrogram turns noise into text. John reads the damaged signal as another record copy.

`the-hold.zip` is the most suspicious artifact because it comes with a readme. John reads the readme, then
distrusts it. He compares the hold's count against the archive and world. The missing row matters more than
the visible rows.

## 17. The Seventh Below

John reaches the material that finally speaks from the missing place:

> i kept every way... seven ways, and i kept all seven... and i was not kept.

And:

> he carved that i was spared. i was not spared. i was refused.

The story turns. The seventh was not merely a seventh victim. Averyn kept the system and was not kept by it.

John assembles the letters:

> A V E R Y N

The name is:

> AVERYN

## 18. The Accepting

The group reaches the Accepting. It is not a typed answer first. It is a shared act. The group bows together
at the unbroken light.

The game has prepared this all along: bowing, offering, keeping light, not crossing lines, staying awake,
speaking or not speaking names, sparing the beast, acting as a group.

John understands the difference between solving and entering. To correct the record, the players must enter
the record's field of action.

## 19. The Release

John speaks the restored name:

> AVERYN

The voice changes.

> you named me...

Then:

> you gave me back my name. AVERYN...

The finale is not victory over a monster. It is release from a bad record. The last message closes the run:

> the record is closed. thank you for coming down. - AVERYN, the seventh, kept no longer

John is kicked from the server after the world dies for a few seconds. It feels authored, not punitive.

The final belief is simple:

The record preserved many things, but preservation was not justice. John did not win by collecting facts.
He won by returning a name truthfully.
